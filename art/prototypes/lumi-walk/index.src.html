Lumi walks Home

Drawings of her at the room’s angle, one for every sixteenth of a turn and one more just past side-on, most of them mirrored. She walks eight ways, rounds corners on the move and turns through the drawings between; code moves the pieces: boots that step, legs drawn to each boot, a cloak that sways and trails a turn. Click the floor to send her somewhere, or let her walk the tour.

What to look at

  • Behind and in front: the room is Home taken apart into an empty floor and its furniture as layers, and a piece is drawn over her only when its footprint reaches lower on the page than her feet. Close behind the low table its top should cover her lower half and never her hood; in front of the cushions, the chair or the armchair nothing of them should cover her; her shadow stays under every piece.
  • A corner of a quarter turn or less, she rounds on the move, turning through the drawings between. A sharper one, she stops, her hood leans toward the new way, and she steps round.
  • Turn: Morph (the default) treats her facing as a place on the ring rather than a drawing: between two drawings it warps each of them row by row until her two edges and the feature between them — the face, or the sun on her hood — land in the same place, then crossfades. Swap is the old behaviour, a hard change of drawing every sixteenth of a turn. Does the morph read as one figure rotating, and does anything tear, ghost or balloon?
  • She faces the way she walks. Turn on the floor plan: a red arrow at her feet marks where her face points (measured on each drawing), a white one where she is walking. The plan also draws the walkable floor (dashed), where her feet may not go (dotted), each piece’s footprint (cyan) and overhang (white), the blockers left in the painting (orange), the proposed anchors (red), and her feet’s width, the columns the depth rule tests (a red bar).
  • Her legs run from under the cloak to her boots every step.
  • Idle: standing she blinks at uneven gaps, now and then glances or tilts her head, sways very slowly, and rarely looks curious or sleepy; walking, she only blinks and glances. Her eyes are drawn over the painted ones, so they can shut, shift and change shape. Try the cues: do the eyes read as hers, or as stickers? Does the tilt bend the collar or tear it?
  • Turning away past side-on, her ribbon bow and medallions shrink to a sliver and go — a drawing just past side-on carries them — rather than fading out.

Her height is in the painting’s own pixels; the table stands about 62. Heights, pace and the turn style here are for judging, not settings in the app.
