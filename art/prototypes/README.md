# art/prototypes — throwaway tests of where Lumi's art could go

Nothing here is cut into `public/` or served by the app. Each prototype tests a bet in `docs/art-direction.md` §8 and is kept so the next session can rebuild it rather than start again. What each one showed goes in `docs/art-direction.md` §4a → *Progress*. Generated outputs are gitignored; the scripts rebuild them from the repo.

## `lumi-pieces/` — the pieces (puppet) test, bets 1 and 2

One drawing moved by code, beside today's whole-drawing sprite. Built 2026-09-13 by a subagent from the hands-free hub; published as the artifact [Lumi pieces](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/36d8ad96-7c2c-44f1-b138-a033e27129b1).

- `split.py` — splits `art/lumi/lumi-free-rest.png`: mattes her off the ground, paints the eyes and their glow out of the face, measures the eyes, face outline, neck pivot and head-weight rows into `rig.json`, and writes `body.png` (the figure at 600px) and `sprite.png` (the comparison sprite's breath rows from `public/lumi-idle.webp`).
- `index.src.html` — the page: two stages (today's sprite, the pieces), sizes 36 · 68 · 150 · 300, paper or a dark room, auto idle, and cues (blink, glances, happy, curious, sleepy, tilt). The head moves on a triangle mesh whose weight falls to zero across the bow, so the collar bends instead of tearing; the eyes are drawn in canvas with the measured colours. `build.py` inlines `body.png`, `sprite.png` and `rig.json` into `index.html`, the file that was published.
- `poses.py` — renders ten poses (`poses.png`), the avatar-size heads (`heads.png`) and close-ups, with the page's maths, to check without a browser.

Rebuild, from the repo root: `python3 art/prototypes/lumi-pieces/split.py && python3 art/prototypes/lumi-pieces/build.py && python3 art/prototypes/lumi-pieces/poses.py`.

**Status:** Chanté, 2026-09-13: "I think lumi pieces looks great", and asked for the walk test below. Before that she was open to puppeting "but still want[s] some sway/breathing motion. Don't want totally static" — the page has a breath but no sway yet. Weak spots it names: the hood tilts as one stiff shape, the eyes slide without foreshortening, the lids are straight cuts, and its breath is a little stronger than the sprite's (2px against 1.44px at 150px).

## `lumi-walk/` — the walk test: a rig walking a painted room, bets 2, 4 and 5

Lumi walking Home's painting, rigged in code from two drawings. Built 2026-09-13 after Chanté approved the pieces; published as the artifact [Lumi walks Home](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/ca116dea-7428-46c9-b11a-8f8275a715ee).

- **The drawings:** five, named by the way she faces on the page — `art/lumi/lumi-iso-front.png` (sw, toward the lower left), `lumi-iso-back.png` (nw, away to the upper left), `lumi-iso-s.png` (toward you), `lumi-iso-w.png` (side-on, left) and `lumi-iso-n.png` (straight away) — generated from the hub and the drawings before them (`art/prompts/lumi-iso-*.md`). sw, w and nw are mirrored for se, e and ne: eight directions from five drawings.
- `split.py` — cuts each into a body (hood, face, cloak, the coat's slit, hands) and two boots, finds the eyes for blinks and writes `rig-walk.json`. The legs are not pieces: each is a hip under the cloak, an ankle at its boot's cuff, a width and a colour, drawn by the page from one to the other every frame. The hem is bridged across the coat's slit by a closing (a running maximum pulled one panel's hem over the far boot). `keep` boxes hold a hand that touches a leg on the body. `debug-split.png` shows each facing whole, tinted by piece (legs green), and pulled apart with the legs following.
- `room-home.json` — Home as a place to walk, in painting px: the walkable floor, each piece of furniture's footprint (for depth and for paths) and its cut-out (laid back over her when she is behind it), and the tour. Measured by eye on 2× zooms; a proposal, not a map.
- `index.src.html` — the page: the rig warps the body row by row in software (bob, squash, sway, lean, the hood's and hem's lag; seamless, and the same in headless Chrome), steps the feet with each boot planted while it carries her and draws the legs to them, and finds paths on a grid of isometric ground that moves only in the eight directions she has drawings for (with a cost per turn, so it turns rarely). At a corner of an eighth of a turn she changes drawing on the move; at a wider one she stops and turns through the drawings between, 0.09 s each, each change carried by a small squash. Click the floor to send her; pace, height, a follow camera and the floor plan for judging. `build.py` inlines the room, pieces and JSON into `index.html`. **Her facing is a float position on the ring (`S.p`), not a drawing:** a turn slides it, `S.dirIdx` is only the drawing nearest to it (which is what the paths, the audit, the floor plan and the readout still read), and between two drawings the page morphs — each drawing warped row by row through `morph.py`'s knots onto the lerp of the two, its legs, boots and ground contacts put on the lerp too, then the nearer of them drawn solid with the other over it at its share. **Turn: Morph** (the default) or **Swap** (the old hard change, for comparison).
- `capture.py` — renders frames headless (`?grid=` steps the tour at a fixed 1/60 s and lays follow-camera cells out in one screenshot) into `out/<name>-grid.png`, a GIF and a strip; `--page` screenshots the whole page; `--audit` runs the page's `?audit=1` mode (the tour and 60 seeded random clicks) and prints how far she walked facing more than 25° off her movement, the worst angle and where, turns by kind, and how far corners were jumped. Motion checked without a browser tab, and by numbers where eyes can't tell. `--trace start,step,count` prints her position, drawing, face and heading at each sample, for when a frame grid is ambiguous.
- `facings.py` — marks where she faces in each drawing (the face; for a back view the turn is judged by eye and recorded in `JUDGED`), prints the turn against what its walking direction needs, writes the turns into `rig-walk.json` for the page, and draws `out/facings.png`: the eight directions in a ring, each with its walking arrow and its face marked. `--candidates <dir>` measures generated candidates before one is chosen (gate 38–52° for a diagonal). Run after `split.py`, before `build.py`.
- `morph.py` — the correspondence a turn morphs through, so the page can hold *the same thing* in two neighbouring drawings. For every row of each drawn facing, from the hood top to the feet line, three x knots: the silhouette's left edge, one feature inside her, the right edge. The feature is the dark void of the face over the hood rows (a view with a face) or the big sun embroidered on it (a view from behind), and the bow and medallions below the hood where a drawing shows her front; rows without one interpolate from the rows that have one and then relax to the silhouette's midpoint, and each column is smoothed vertically so no row disagrees with the row above it. Each facing is marked `front` or `back`, and the page refuses to pair a face with a sun — between side-on and the first view from behind the morph falls back to the two edges alone. Written to `morph.json`; `out/morph-knots.png` draws the three curves over each drawing. Mirrored facings need no data (the page negates x and swaps left and right). Run after `split.py`, before `build.py`.

Rebuild, from the repo root: `python3 art/prototypes/lumi-walk/split.py && python3 art/prototypes/lumi-walk/facings.py && python3 art/prototypes/lumi-walk/morph.py && python3 art/prototypes/lumi-walk/build.py`, then `python3 art/prototypes/lumi-walk/capture.py --plan --zoom 1.6 --cell 480x320 --step 2.5 --count 20 --cols 5 --start 0 --name tour`. `capture.py --turn swap` renders the same frames with the old behaviour, for comparison.

**Status:** open, awaiting Chanté's eye. What the renders showed before publishing: boots step and hold, the tabletop, lantern and plant cover her behind the table and she covers the cushions in front; a blink first left the painted eyes' rims as orange rings (the lid now takes the rim and glow). Known limits: a turn is a dip and a swap, not an in-between; one room, one tour; nothing in the app plays it.

**Chanté's first look (2026-09-13):** "This is looking great! She's clipping the table, so we'll need to work on our mapping. But she looks awesome!" The rig passed; the map didn't. Two mistakes, both fixed in `room-home.json` and the page: the table's footprint was traced from its legs' feet, but the top overhangs them by ~15px (it is now the tabletop's outline dropped by its 65px height); and the clearance kept her *feet* 11px off a footprint while her cloak reaches ~25px either side (now 24px). Still true after the fix: close behind a low table the tabletop covers most of her, because at this painting's angle a 65px table hides a lot of floor — hand-traced polygons and a bounding-box depth test are the weak part, not the drawings.

**Chanté's second look (2026-09-13):** "When she turns, she's walking sideways like a crab · The turning is abrupt, it's a 'flash! now she's facing this way' · As she's walking her legs sometimes disappear, so her feet are walking nicely, but they appear to be floating beneath her. Same with her body beneath the slit of her coat." Three causes, three fixes: (1) she walked at any angle with four diagonal drawings, and at a corner she arrived still moving at 14% pace, so the stop-then-turn check never fired and she walked the next leg in the old drawing — now paths move only in eight drawn directions and she stops fully before any wider turn; (2) a turn was one swap — now three more drawings (s, w, n) give every turn drawings to pass through; (3) each foot piece carried the painted leg above its boot and the dark of the coat's slit, so a step pulled them away — now the feet are boots alone, the slit stays on the body, and the legs are drawn. Also found on the way: `build.py` inlined only the old two facings, so the first grid was black.

**Chanté's third look (2026-09-13):** "She's still not always walking forward · When she does a walking turn it's good. But when she's standing still she flips instantly · Her feet are a little better, but still clip a bit" (with a screenshot: facing the viewer, one boot gone under the cloak). Causes and fixes: (1) the short stretches joining her real start and end to the path grid ran at any angle — now joined by moves in the eight directions, and any stretch under 6px is dropped; (2) a turn from standing was one or two swaps 0.09s apart with nothing moving — now each eighth of a turn is a small step on the spot, 0.2s, the drawing changing as the boot lifts, her weight shifting; (3) pace and stride were set in page pixels, so up and down the page (where the ground is foreshortened) she over-strode and the back boot slid under the hem — now both are set on the ground, and a boot may tuck up toward the hem but never under it. **Checked by numbers:** `capture.py --audit` walks the tour for two minutes and 60 random clicks and measures facing against movement every frame — before the last fix 36px of 30,600 walked sideways (worst 180°, a 5px stretch walked backwards) and 678px of corner jumps; after, 0px sideways (worst 24.6°), no turn from standing without a step (173 stepped turns, 105 on the move), 38px of jumps in all.

**Chanté's fourth look (2026-09-13):** "The biggest issue is she's still not always walking forward. I feel like it should be a relatively easy task of marking where her face is and always making sure it's facing in the direction she's walking." She was right, and the audit had missed it: it proved she moved along the direction each drawing was *assigned*, never that the drawing faced that way. `facings.py` marks the face in each drawing: the two diagonal drawings were turned only ~29° (front) and well under 45° (back) where the room's diagonals need 45°, and those diagonals are the room's main walking lines. Both were redrawn (prompt v2: "exactly halfway between" the attached facing-you, or facing-away, and side-on drawings). The front's four candidates all measured 41–46° (v1's three: 26–28°); the back has no face, and neither the back's sun nor the line between the boots could be trusted, so it was chosen by eye in a strip between facing away and side-on, and its 45° is recorded as judged. The page now reads each drawing's measured turn: the audit judges her against where the face really points (per-direction error 0–5°), and the floor plan draws a red face arrow and a white walking arrow at her feet.

**Chanté's fifth look (2026-09-13):** "That's looking better. Turning could still be smoother though." Every change of drawing was a 45° jump. Now **sixteen facings, 22.5° apart**: four in-between drawings (`lumi-iso-ssw`, `-wsw`, `-wnw`, `-nnw`) join the five, and with mirroring she has a drawing every sixteenth of a turn; she still walks only the eight even ones. In code: corners of a quarter turn or less are rounded (a short curve she walks, changing drawing on the move); sharper turns stop, the hood leans toward the new way first, and she steps round with the first and last changes eased longer; the hem swings out behind each change on a spring; and a **Turn: Swap / Blend** toggle fades the new drawing in over 70 ms for comparison. Tour stops with no rest join the next leg so she rounds them. The in-betweens: "exactly halfway between" overshot for the 22.5° drawing (eight candidates at 32–39°), so v3 asked for an edit of the facing-you drawing and landed 12–17°; the 67.5° front one passed at 62.6°; the two from behind were judged by eye. Audit after: 0px sideways (worst 17.7°), no turns from standing without a step, every facing within 5° of its measured face.

**Chanté's sixth look (2026-09-13):** "Swap appears slightly better, but it's still pretty choppy." Sixteen drawings had made the steps smaller without making them steps at all: neighbouring drawings differ in more than angle — the hood is a different width, the cloak falls differently, the face sits somewhere else — so every change was still a jump, and the crossfade showed both at once. Now a turn is **a morph, not a change of drawing**. `morph.py` measures, per row, what in one drawing is the same thing as what in the next — the two silhouette edges and one feature between them (the face; from behind, the sun on her hood) — and the page's warp gained a piecewise-linear x-remap through those three knots, on top of the vertical remap, sway, breath, hood lean and hem swing it already did. Her facing became a float on the ring: a standing turn slides it the whole way on one ease at the old pace (the sixteenths still cost `turnStep` each, the first and last longer), a corner is rounded by sliding it over ~0.15 s, and the squash that used to hide each swap is gone because there is no longer a pop to hide. Two things the morph forced, both improvements: a turn that takes time has to *start before the corner*, so a rounded corner now begins half a turn-step early and she walks it as an arc halfway between her facing and the path (before, the facing snapped exactly when the heading did, which is why the old audit read 0 px sideways); and the stepping feet are now indexed by which foot is on the *screen* left rather than which is further away, because four of the sixteen pairs of neighbours order their two legs the other way round and a morph would have pulled leg onto leg. **Checked in the frames** (`capture.py --start 3.0 --step 0.033 --count 32 --cols 8 --zoom 8 --cell 300x300`, and `--turn swap` for the same window): the hood rotates, the face narrows and slides off to the side, the medallions turn — one figure rotating, where Swap holds a pose and then jumps. What is still not perfect: where the two drawings genuinely hold different things the crossfade still shows both for ~0.1 s — the bow and medallions ghost through as she passes side-on into the first view from behind, which is also the one pair whose feature knot has to be dropped (a face and a sun are opposite sides of her) — and mid-morph the two silhouettes miss each other by a pixel or two at the outline, a faint doubled contour at 14× zoom that is invisible at her real height. **By numbers:** 0 px of 30,748 walked sideways (worst 22.9°), no turn from standing without a step, 350 stepped turns and 249 on the move.

## `home-layers/` — Home as an empty plate plus furniture layers, bet 5

Home's painting taken apart instead of mapped by hand. Chanté, asked whether to refine the map or rebuild the rooms from layered items, chose to **cut the furniture out of the existing painting and inpaint the floor behind it**. Built 2026-09-13 on `ux/home-layers`; not published, nothing served.

- `room.src.json` — the hand-traced source, in painting px: the **inventory** (every floor-standing thing on or beside the floor, kept or left in the plate, with the reason), and per layer its `body` polygons (hugging the silhouette ~2px outside; several for thin things like the chair and balustrade), `foliage` polygons (leaves, fringes), the `mask` sent to the API, `footprint` (where it meets the floor), `extent` (the widest part dropped to the floor), `height` and proposed `anchors`; the `blockers` left in the plate; the `floor`; the chosen inpaint candidate per cluster. Traced on grid zooms from `zoom.py`, checked on `masks.py`'s overlay (`out/masks.png`, `--zoom x0,y0,x1,y1,k`).
- `inpaint.py <A|B|C|D>` — one cluster (the table and cushions; armchair and stool; desk and chair; balustrade) through OpenAI's `/v1/images/edits` with a mask, `gpt-image-2.5-sunburst`, 1536×1024, high; keeps the hole's neighbourhood of each result in `out/inpaint/` and logs tokens and cost. The rug needed a second prompt: the first invented a central medallion.
- `plate.py` — writes `art/scenery/home/plate.png`, `layers/<id>.png` and `<id>-shadow.png`, and `layers.json`, and prints the recompose error. The inpaint is used only under what a layer covers, colour-corrected to its surroundings. The floor an item only shades keeps the painting's own pixels, relit (the shadow is the luminance ratio against the inpaint, smoothed); that darkening becomes the black `-shadow` layer. A layer is solid inside its polygons; its edge is a colour-line matte between the item's own colour and the floor just outside. Leaves are a difference matte against the inpaint. Colour is de-matted against the plate. The docstring has the details.
- `depth.py` — the depth rule, clearance and the walkable floor, with self-tests. In one page column two ground points differ only in u + v, so **an item draws over Lumi when its ground polygon has a point in her feet columns (x ± 17) lower on the page than her feet**. An item with ground only within her drawing's reach (x ± 37) is tested at its column nearest her. Layers and Lumi are ordered by a topological sort. Walkable = `floor` minus every extent grown by her clearance as a ground circle (an ellipse 24 × 13.7 px).
- `cut_lumi.py` — a stand-in Lumi, `art/lumi/lumi-iso-s.png` matted with `scripts/lumi_cut.py`, at 2× her 120px.
- `index.src.html` + `build.py` → `index.html` — the viewer, in the walk page's style. It shows the plate, shadows and layers with Lumi depth-sorted; click or drag to place her. Views: Composed · Plate only · Layers tinted · Original painting (a cut you slide between the painting and the rebuilt room), plus Footprints and anchors. `build.py` makes the test points: behind, in front of, left and right of each layer, a row close behind the low table, and behind the balustrade posts. It also prints any layer whose pixels reach past its ground polygon.
- `capture.py` — headless Chrome: the default `?grid=` mode puts Lumi at every test point in one screenshot (`out/grid.png`; `--view tinted`), `--test` checks the JS rule against `depth.py`, `--page` screenshots the page.

Rebuild, from the repo root, from the committed plate and layers: `python3 art/prototypes/home-layers/cut_lumi.py && python3 art/prototypes/home-layers/build.py`, then `python3 art/prototypes/home-layers/capture.py --test`, `capture.py`, `capture.py --view tinted --name grid-tinted`, `capture.py --page`. To rebuild the plate itself, first generate candidates with `inpaint.py <cluster> --n 2` (about $0.11 a run; `.env.local`'s key), name the chosen ones in `room.src.json` → `plate_candidates`, then run `plate.py` before the above. The candidates are gitignored.

**Status:** built, awaiting Chanté's eye. **Recompose** (plate × shadows, then layers, from the quantised files, against the painting; per channel, 0–255):
- whole painting: mean 0.030, max 121;
- inside the holes: mean 0.13, with 0.52% of pixels off by more than 8;
- per mask (mean / max / share off by more than 8): table 0.10 / 109 / 0.30%, cream cushion 0.08 / 70 / 0.20%, green cushion 0.06 / 29 / 0.10%, armchair 0.05 / 36 / 0.06%, stool 0.08 / 20 / 0.07%, desk 0.08 / 78 / 0.20%, chair 0.12 / 53 / 0.74%, balustrade 0.19 / 121 / 0.95%.

These numbers are small largely by construction (de-matting is exact wherever a layer has alpha); they catch overlaps, clipping and lost pixels, not ugly seams. The JS rule agrees with `depth.py` on 248 depth cases and 722 walkable points.

What the grids show:
- **Order is right.** In front of every layer nothing covers her. Behind the table the top covers her lower half, including close behind its back edge where the hand-traced map failed. Behind the balustrade posts the post tops cross her feet. The desk no longer cuts her once its top's overhang has an extent.

**Known issues:**
- (1) Close behind the table, a few one-pixel teeth of her cloak show through the tabletop's back-left edge.
- (2) The table's plant and the desk's plant are difference-matted against an inpaint whose rug (or wall) doesn't match the painting's, so they carry rug flecks and a cream flower over her hood.
- (3) The chair carries a strip of floor between its right post and the throw.
- (4) Level with a footprint corner, the corner covers a few px of her hem.
- (5) **Plate only** has seams hugging every silhouette: the rug's flowers break along the table's outline, and dark ghosts of its front legs remain. Plank lines and the window's light patches don't line up under the chair and by the armchair. Under the balustrade the plate is a patchwork of floor and stair. None of this shows with the layers on.
- (6) The stair, the right-hand bookcase and the log basket stay in the plate as blockers.
- (7) The floor's front edge by the balustrade is a guess (the rails run down a stair, so floor level there is ambiguous).
- (8) Anchors are proposals.
- (9) API: 10 images logged at about $0.54, plus two requests that hung and were killed (possibly billed, about $0.22 more).

## `shadow-mock.py` — Lumi's shadows on the room paintings

The before and after behind *Lumi's shadow is drawn by the page* (`docs/decisions.md`, 2026-09-13): the companion's CSS contact shadow, cast shadow and room light reproduced in numpy on the Today and Library paintings at her spots, beside the baked cream shadow they replaced.
