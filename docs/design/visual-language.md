## Purpose

This document defines the visual language of Coherence: the relationship between environment, interface, typography, materials, colour, imagery, restraint, and character presence across Home, Garden, Library, and Study.

The visual system should support the product philosophy rather than decorate it.

The central visual principle is:

Environmental richness can carry atmosphere and continuity while the functional interface remains quiet, crisp, and cognitively light.

The current direction can be summarized as:

antique book × inhabited world × crisp modern interface

This is not a literal style recipe. It is a tension to preserve.

Coherence should feel timeless without feeling costume-like, warm without becoming sentimental, distinctive without becoming busy, and magical without becoming fantasy software.

## 1. The visual language must reduce cognitive load

Aesthetic ambition is secondary to orientation.

The first question for any screen is not:

Is this beautiful?

It is:

Does the user immediately understand where to look and what matters?

Beauty in Coherence should support hierarchy, containment, familiarity, and emotional ease.

If an aesthetic choice makes the interface harder to parse, it is probably wrong.

## 2. The world can be rich because the interface is restrained

Coherence deliberately separates two visual layers.

The environmental layer can contain:\
- architecture\
- landscape\
- materials\
- accumulated history\
- atmospheric lighting\
- signs of inhabitation\
- Lumi

The functional layer should remain:\
- sparse\
- legible\
- typographically clear\
- low-noise\
- predictable

The environment creates place.

The interface creates clarity.

They should complement one another rather than compete.

## 3. Avoid decorating the interface to make it feel warm

Warmth should not come from filling cards and controls with decorative imagery.

Avoid patterns such as:\
- coffee cups beside tasks\
- stacks of books on cards\
- little plants beside reminders\
- wellness illustrations\
- hand-drawn doodles surrounding controls\
- decorative inspirational imagery

These choices can quickly push Coherence toward a generic cozy-productivity aesthetic.

The environment and Lumi already provide personality.

The UI does not need to perform warmth separately.

## 4. Avoid “live laugh love” visual language

Coherence should explicitly avoid the visual vocabulary of lifestyle wellness apps.

This includes:\
- motivational posters\
- soft-focus lifestyle scenes\
- pastel affirmation graphics\
- decorative mugs and blankets\
- handwritten inspirational typography\
- arbitrary botanical ornament around text\
- precious decorative quotes

The product should feel thoughtful and inhabited, not curated for a lifestyle mood board.

## 5. Avoid teenage-cute visual language

Lumi can be endearing without making the whole product feel juvenile.

Avoid:\
- bubble-heavy UI\
- candy colours\
- exaggerated rounded cards everywhere\
- hearts and sparkles as general ornament\
- overly expressive stickers\
- kawaii-style interface treatment\
- playful typography that competes with content

The surrounding design should be mature enough that Lumi’s smallness and charm feel intentional rather than childish.

## 6. Avoid generic SaaS cleanliness

The opposite failure is also possible.

Coherence should not become:\
- flat white dashboard\
- blue gradient\
- generic sans-serif everything\
- floating cards on light grey\
- standard productivity icons\
- visual identity carried only by logo colour

The product needs a world and a point of view.

Clarity should not require visual anonymity.

## 7. The antique-book influence is about material intelligence

“Antique book” should not mean placing parchment textures everywhere.

The useful qualities are:\
- typographic hierarchy\
- editorial restraint\
- warm paper-like neutrals\
- fine rules and borders\
- subtle ornament\
- careful margins\
- tactile materiality\
- quiet age\
- accumulated use\
- dignified imperfection

The reference is less “old-timey interface” and more:

What if this digital system had the visual discipline and intimacy of a beautifully made old book?

## 8. Do not overuse paper texture

Texture can add material depth, but too much immediately becomes costume.

Avoid:\
- obvious scanned paper grain across every surface\
- stained edges\
- fake torn paper\
- excessive sepia\
- distressed type\
- faux leather everywhere

Prefer subtle material suggestion.

The user should feel texture before consciously noticing it.

## 9. The modern layer should remain genuinely modern

Coherence is not historical fantasy.

Modern clarity should appear through:\
- responsive layouts\
- precise spacing\
- excellent typography\
- crisp interaction states\
- strong accessibility\
- clean forms\
- restrained controls\
- smooth motion\
- minimal friction

The old-world material layer and modern interaction layer should create productive tension.

## 10. Typography should carry much of the identity

Typography is one of the most important visual tools in Coherence because it can provide character without adding clutter.

The system should likely combine:\
- an editorial or bookish display/serif voice\
- a highly readable interface/body voice

The exact typefaces remain open.

The goal is not “serif = antique.”

The goal is a typographic system that feels literate, calm, intentional, and durable.

## 11. Typography should remain quiet

Avoid too many font styles, decorative alternates, or dramatic scales.

The hierarchy should feel editorial rather than promotional.

A useful direction:\
- restrained page titles\
- clear section headings\
- calm body copy\
- subtle metadata\
- strong but infrequent emphasis

The product should not look like a marketing landing page once the user is inside it.

## 12. Interface text should be readable before atmospheric

If an environment has visual complexity, UI typography needs enough contrast and visual separation to remain immediately readable.

Never sacrifice text clarity to make interface elements “blend into the scene.”

The functional layer should feel integrated with the world without becoming camouflaged by it.

## 13. Colour should feel natural, aged, and intelligent

The palette should likely centre on:\
- warm ivory\
- parchment neutrals\
- muted stone\
- warm charcoal\
- mossy or botanical greens\
- desaturated blues\
- antique gold\
- occasional earthy accents

These are directional families, not fixed tokens.

Avoid overly saturated UI colour unless it communicates actual state or urgency.

## 14. Gold should remain an accent

Antique gold is already important in Lumi’s design and the broader world.

It should signal:\
- ornament\
- continuity\
- subtle importance\
- celestial or archival motifs

It should not become a luxury-brand coating applied to everything.

Too much gold would make Coherence feel ornate, premium-coded, or fantasy-like.

Use it sparingly enough that it retains meaning.

## 15. Black should rarely be absolute black

Warm dark tones will likely fit the world better than hard digital black.

This can help the visual language feel softer and more material without reducing contrast.

Accessibility requirements remain primary.

## 16. White should rarely be sterile white

Functional surfaces may benefit from warm light neutrals rather than pure white.

Again, subtlety matters.

The interface should not look yellowed or vintage for its own sake.

## 17. The spaces should share one visual ancestry

Home, Garden, Library, and Study must feel like parts of one world.

Shared qualities may include:\
- typography\
- material families\
- ornament language\
- architectural logic\
- light behaviour\
- age/history\
- recurring motifs\
- Lumi

Each space can have a distinct emotional register without looking like a separate themed app.

## 18. Home should feel familiar, interior, and low-demand

Home should visually communicate:\
- arrival\
- safety\
- familiarity\
- contemplation\
- quiet continuity

It may be one of the most emotionally inhabited spaces.

Avoid making it visually busy simply because it is the landing page.

Home is not a dashboard.

## 19. The Garden should feel open and alive

The Garden can carry more environmental movement and organic irregularity than the other spaces.

It should communicate:\
- cultivation\
- time\
- openness\
- selective care\
- life continuing beyond the interface

A mature Garden can be rich without turning into a botanical illustration around task cards.

## 20. The Library should feel accumulated, navigable, and quietly mysterious

The Library can communicate breadth and history.

It should feel:\
- established\
- layered\
- archival\
- warm\
- intelligent\
- retrievable

It should not feel cluttered or visually impenetrable.

Its richness should increase confidence that things can be found, not anxiety that there is too much.

## 21. The Study should be the quietest space

The Study should visually narrow attention.

Compared with the Garden and Library, it should generally have:\
- fewer moving elements\
- less visible information\
- more stable composition\
- stronger containment\
- generous empty space

The visual message is:

Nothing else is asking for you here.

## 22. Visual density should correspond to cognitive mode

The spaces can differ meaningfully in density.

Home: low-demand openness.

Garden: selective foreground against environmental richness.

Library: broader visible structure with progressive disclosure.

Study: extreme reduction.

This variation is functional, not merely stylistic.

## 23. Empty space is active design

Whitespace is not unfinished design.

In Coherence it communicates:\
- hierarchy\
- permission to focus\
- reduced demand\
- confidence\
- containment

Avoid filling empty areas because the screen appears visually unbalanced.

Sometimes the correct design is simply quieter.

## 24. Cards should not become the default answer

Modern interface design often turns every concept into a rounded rectangle.

Coherence should resist card proliferation.

Cards are useful when they provide meaningful grouping or interaction boundaries.

They should not be used merely because every piece of information needs a container.

The page itself can provide hierarchy through spacing, typography, and composition.

## 25. Rounded corners should be restrained

Excessive large-radius rounding pushes the product toward contemporary wellness/SaaS aesthetics.

Coherence may use subtle rounding where useful, but edges can also be:\
- lightly rounded\
- square\
- rule-based\
- framed editorially

The exact system can evolve.

The important point is not to default to soft-pill everything.

## 26. Borders can carry editorial character

Fine rules, frames, dividers, and subtle linework may fit Coherence well.

They can reference:\
- book plates\
- marginalia\
- archival labels\
- old diagrams\
- fine printing

Use restraint.

The goal is structure, not ornament for ornament’s sake.

## 27. Ornament should have lineage

Recurring ornaments can help create a world.

Potential motifs may include:\
- celestial forms\
- small suns\
- stars\
- botanical geometry\
- archival marks\
- fine line symbols

Lumi’s gold sun ornament already establishes part of this vocabulary.

New ornament should feel related rather than randomly decorative.

## 28. Symbolism should remain subtle

Coherence’s themes include cultivation, illumination, memory, continuity, and coherence across scales.

The visual system can quietly echo these ideas.

Avoid turning them into obvious logos everywhere.

The strongest symbolism is often discovered rather than announced.

## 29. Icons should be simple and elegant

Functional icons should be:\
- immediately understandable\
- visually restrained\
- consistent in weight\
- small enough not to dominate\
- compatible with the editorial/material world

Avoid cartoonish icon sets or highly detailed pictograms.

## 30. Avoid decorative scene fragments inside functional UI

If the user is looking at a task surface, do not place miniature scenes inside every panel.

The environment already carries place.

Functional elements should remain functional.

This separation is one of the strongest defences against visual clutter.

## 31. The interface should appear placed within the world, not pasted on top

Although the functional layer is distinct, it should not feel like a generic web app floating above an illustration.

Integration can come through:\
- shared light temperature\
- compatible materials\
- consistent edge language\
- typography\
- spacing\
- subtle shadows\
- recurring ornament

The environment and UI should feel designed together.

## 32. Depth should remain gentle

Avoid highly glossy glassmorphism, dramatic shadows, or floating 3D panels.

A subtle sense of layering is enough.

The world should feel tactile, not futuristic.

## 33. Shadows should suggest material rather than spectacle

Where shadows are used, they should be soft and restrained.

The aim is to establish hierarchy and physicality.

Not to create visual effects.

## 34. Lighting can distinguish spaces

Lighting may become one of the strongest ways to differentiate environments while preserving one world.

For example:\
- Home: familiar warm ambient light\
- Garden: natural changing light\
- Library: deeper warm archival light\
- Study: contained, steady task light

These are conceptual directions, not fixed rules.

## 35. Time-of-day variation can support inhabitation

Subtle time-of-day shifts may eventually deepen continuity.

They should not reduce legibility or radically alter the interface.

The user should feel the world has a relationship to time without needing to manage a dynamic theme system.

## 36. Environmental history should be slow and believable

As spaces evolve, they should feel as though they have accumulated rather than unlocked.

Visual change should generally be:\
- gradual\
- contextual\
- subtle\
- additive without becoming crowded

The user may occasionally notice:

Oh. That’s new.

Avoid achievement-style announcements.

## 37. Visual growth should not indicate moral success

A richer environment must not imply a better or more productive person.

Sparse should not mean failing.

Rich should not mean disciplined.

Environmental depth should represent continuity, history, and accumulated context rather than performance.

## 38. Absence should never visibly punish the user

Do not make environments:\
- decay\
- die\
- gather guilt-coded dust\
- become dark\
- lose objects\
- show neglect warnings

because the user stopped opening Coherence.

The world should welcome return.

## 39. Lumi is the primary source of visible personality

The interface can remain restrained because Lumi carries character.

Her:\
- silhouette\
- movement\
- presence\
- reactions\
- ornament\
- positioning

provide warmth and recognition.

This is preferable to spreading cuteness across the entire design system.

## 40. Lumi should remain visually distinct from UI controls

She is a character, not an icon button.

Her position and scale should make it clear when she is:\
- inhabiting the space\
- reacting\
- available for interaction

versus when the user is operating functional UI.

Do not turn every Lumi interaction into clicking her body.

## 41. Lumi’s scale is part of her charm

Lumi’s smallness should be preserved.

She should feel like a little inhabitant of a larger world rather than a giant mascot dominating screens.

Her scale also allows environmental space to remain emotionally important.

## 42. Lumi’s visual simplicity should be protected

Her power comes from a simple silhouette:\
- oversized cream/ivory hooded cloak\
- dark face\
- glowing oval eyes\
- small visible feet\
- warm gold details

Avoid feature creep such as:\
- too many facial expressions\
- accessories for every state\
- decorative costume variants\
- exaggerated props

Small changes can carry enough expression.

## 43. The eyes do substantial emotional work

Because Lumi’s face is minimal, eye position, timing, and orientation can communicate:\
- attention\
- curiosity\
- amusement\
- thoughtfulness\
- presence

This should remain understated.

## 44. Material consistency matters

Lumi’s soft tactile rendering should belong naturally within the broader world.

If she looks handmade and textured while the environment is glossy vector art, the world will fragment.

Likewise, if the environment is painterly but the UI is aggressively flat and synthetic, integration suffers.

## 45. The world should feel illustrated, not stock-illustrated

Avoid generic stock-art visual language.

Environmental art should feel authored and specific to Coherence.

The spaces should develop recognizable visual identity rather than looking like interchangeable cozy-background assets.

## 46. Avoid literal productivity imagery

Do not visually communicate productivity through:\
- checkmarks everywhere\
- clocks everywhere\
- clipboards\
- charts\
- stacks of paperwork\
- generic desk scenes

The product’s environments are about relationships to attention, not illustrated office work.

## 47. Avoid fantasy-RPG signalling

The environmental world can be magical without becoming game-like.

Avoid:\
- quest markers\
- inventory frames\
- glowing loot\
- level-up effects\
- ornate fantasy UI\
- RPG map language\
- health/mana-like meters

Coherence is inhabited software, not a productivity game.

## 48. Avoid dark-academia cosplay

The Library creates an obvious risk of slipping into dark-academia clichés.

Avoid overreliance on:\
- candles everywhere\
- skulls\
- endless mahogany\
- gothic lettering\
- stacks of ancient tomes as decoration\
- overly moody brown-black palettes

The Library should feel archival and mysterious without becoming a themed set.

## 49. Avoid cottagecore cosplay

Likewise, the Garden should not become a cottagecore mood board.

Avoid defaulting to:\
- baskets\
- teacups\
- gingham\
- rustic signs\
- tiny gardening tools\
- decorative mushrooms everywhere

The Garden metaphor is about cultivation and selective care.

Its visual language can be more restrained and timeless.

## 50. Nostalgia should feel structural, not referential

Coherence can feel nostalgic without imitating a specific decade.

Nostalgia can come from:\
- material tactility\
- familiarity\
- accumulated history\
- quiet domestic scale\
- old-world editorial discipline\
- recurring places

Avoid overt retro gimmicks unless they serve a specific design purpose.

## 51. Familiarity should grow through repetition

The same environmental anchors should recur enough that the user learns the spaces.

Examples:\
- characteristic architecture\
- repeated furniture or structural forms\
- recognizable paths\
- stable lighting patterns\
- Lumi’s usual places

This supports the feeling:

I know where I am.

## 52. Visual change should preserve recognition

As environments expand or evolve, foundational composition should remain recognizable.

The user should feel that the same place has developed.

Not that the app swapped in a new background.

## 53. Navigation should remain plain enough to understand

*Current label decision: Library remains the user-facing name already chosen. Functional labels below illustrate clarity, not a requirement to rename it Lists. Other navigation choices remain open.*

Environmental world-building should not force navigation labels into metaphorical obscurity.

Primary navigation can remain:\
- Home\
- Today\
- Lists\
- Focus

The world can communicate:\
- Home\
- Garden\
- Library\
- Study

through experience.

Clarity comes first.

## 54. Transitions can imply geography without becoming cinematic

Movement between spaces may use:\
- shifting light\
- slight parallax\
- architectural continuity\
- Lumi walking a short distance\
- crossfades\
- material transitions

Avoid long animated travel sequences.

The user should never wait for the world-building.

## 55. Responsive design should preserve cognitive hierarchy

On mobile, Coherence should not attempt to shrink desktop environments literally.

Preserve:\
- the current cognitive mode\
- Lumi’s presence\
- primary hierarchy\
- material/typographic identity

The environmental layer may become more suggestive and cropped.

The functional hierarchy must remain intact.

## 56. Accessibility is part of the visual language

The world must support:\
- adequate contrast\
- clear text sizing\
- keyboard navigation\
- non-drag alternatives\
- reduced motion\
- readable focus states\
- screen-reader structure

Accessibility should not be treated as an exception to the aesthetic.

A mature visual language includes accessible states from the beginning.

## 57. Reduced motion should still feel inhabited

Users who prefer reduced motion should not receive a visually dead version of Coherence.

Presence can remain through:\
- static pose changes\
- gentle opacity changes\
- environmental composition\
- light shifts\
- restrained state transitions

Motion is one tool for inhabitation, not the only one.

## 58. High-contrast and system modes should preserve identity

If alternate accessibility or display modes are introduced, they should preserve enough typography, spacing, ornament, and material logic that the product still feels like Coherence.

Identity should not depend on low contrast or subtle textures.

## 59. Visual states should communicate actual meaning

Colour and emphasis should represent meaningful states such as:\
- true urgency\
- selected/current attention\
- disabled/unavailable\
- active focus\
- error

Avoid using warning colours merely because something is incomplete or old.

Visual urgency should map to real-world urgency, not guilt.

## 60. Completion should be visually restrained

Avoid confetti, explosions, badges, and exaggerated completion animations.

Completion may be communicated through:\
- gentle removal\
- subtle transition\
- a small check\
- Lumi’s understated reaction\
- receding emphasis

The product should feel satisfying without creating reward loops.

## 61. Micro-interactions should feel tactile and precise

Small interactions can reinforce the material world.

Dragging, placing, expanding, opening, and focusing should feel:\
- smooth\
- deliberate\
- responsive\
- lightly physical

Avoid springy toy-like motion unless a particular Lumi animation genuinely calls for it.

## 62. Dragging should communicate structure visually

In the Library, drag interactions should make hierarchy and destination clear.

The visual language should show:\
- where an item will land\
- how priority changes\
- when context/category changes

The interaction should feel intuitive enough that users do not need instructions.

## 63. Progressive disclosure should also be visual

The Library’s zoom model and the Garden’s hierarchy require a visual grammar for revealing more detail only when relevant.

At distance:\
- broad structure

Closer:\
- more labels\
- more context

Selected:\
- full interaction

This is preferable to showing all metadata everywhere.

## 64. Environmental composition should leave room for UI

Background art should be designed with functional overlays in mind.

Do not create a beautiful scene and then force interface text over visually complex regions.

The composition should intentionally provide quiet zones.

## 65. The UI should not permanently obscure the world

Likewise, functional surfaces should not cover so much of the environment that the spatial metaphor becomes irrelevant.

The amount of environment visible can vary by mode, but place should remain perceptible where it matters.

## 66. The world is not wallpaper

Environmental art should contribute to:\
- cognitive mode\
- continuity\
- metaphor\
- emotional tone\
- navigation

If removing the environment changes nothing except prettiness, the design may not yet be doing enough conceptual work.

## 67. But the world should not become required puzzle-solving

The opposite is also true.

Users should not need to decode environmental symbolism to operate the product.

The world can deepen meaning while functional controls remain plain.

## 68. Visual language should support containment

Containment has emerged as an important Coherence principle.

Visual design can reinforce it through:\
- clear boundaries around the current foreground\
- reduced surrounding contrast\
- stable composition\
- generous negative space\
- quiet backgrounding of non-current elements

The user should feel that attention has somewhere safe to land.

## 69. The visual hierarchy should help the user stop scanning

A successful Coherence screen should not invite constant eye movement across competing modules.

Where possible, composition should say:

Look here.

Everything else is being held.

This is particularly important in Garden and Study.

## 70. Visual complexity should never exceed conceptual complexity

Do not give a simple concept a complicated visual treatment.

If the user only needs to choose between two actions, the interface should not stage that as an elaborate scene.

World-building should enrich the product without making simple decisions feel ceremonial.

## 71. Coherence should feel calm without becoming bland

Calm is not the absence of identity.

Distinctiveness can come from:\
- material language\
- typography\
- Lumi\
- environmental composition\
- subtle symbolism\
- accumulated place

The product should feel quiet and unmistakably itself.

## 72. Coherence should feel warm without becoming soft everywhere

Warmth can coexist with:\
- crisp alignment\
- strong hierarchy\
- dark text\
- precise controls\
- direct language

Do not equate warmth with roundedness, pastel colour, or sentimentality.

## 73. Coherence should feel magical without claiming magic

The world may carry a quiet sense of wonder through:\
- Lumi\
- light\
- scale\
- subtle environmental change\
- archival mystery\
- celestial ornament

Avoid glowing-everything fantasy language.

Magic should feel like atmosphere, not special effects.

## 74. Coherence should feel personal without requiring customization

The environment can become increasingly personal through accumulated context and history.

This is different from asking users to choose:\
- wallpaper\
- furniture\
- theme packs\
- decorative items

Customization may exist later, but personalization should primarily emerge from relationship and use.

## 75. The interface should age well

Avoid aesthetics tightly tied to one design trend.

The antique/editorial influence may help because it is rooted in durable visual principles rather than novelty.

Design decisions should ask:

Will this still feel intentional in five years?

## 76. The visual system should be tokenizable

Although this document is conceptual, implementation should eventually codify:\
- colour roles\
- spacing\
- typography scale\
- border/rule treatments\
- shadow levels\
- corner treatments\
- icon weights\
- environmental overlay behaviour

The goal is consistency without erasing the individuality of spaces.

## 77. Design tokens should describe function, not just colour names

Prefer conceptual roles such as:\
- surface-primary\
- surface-quiet\
- text-primary\
- text-muted\
- accent-gold\
- urgency-real\
- focus-current\
- environment-overlay

rather than hard-coding visual values by screen.

This allows the system to evolve while preserving meaning.

## 78. Space-specific palettes should remain related

Each environment may emphasize different parts of the broader palette.

Home may be warmer.

Garden may introduce more living greens and natural light.

Library may deepen neutrals and gold.

Study may reduce variety and stabilize contrast.

They should still feel genetically related.

## 79. Product screenshots should look recognizably coherent

A useful practical test:

Place screenshots of Home, Garden, Library, and Study side by side with labels removed.

They should clearly belong to one product.

They should also clearly represent different cognitive environments.

If either condition fails, the visual system needs adjustment.

## 80. Visual review questions

When reviewing a design, ask:

Does the eye know where to go first?

Is this screen quieter than the user’s likely internal state?

Is warmth coming from meaningful place and Lumi, or decorative clutter?

Does the interface look like generic SaaS with an illustration behind it?

Does the antique influence feel material and editorial, or costume-like?

Have we slipped into wellness-app, cottagecore, dark-academia, fantasy-RPG, or kawaii clichés?

Is the environment carrying conceptual meaning?

Is the functional UI still crisp and immediately legible?

Are empty areas allowed to remain empty?

Are visual changes communicating real state rather than moral pressure?

Does this belong to the same world as the other spaces?

Could a user with reduced motion or accessibility needs still receive the core identity?

Does the visual system make the user hold less?

## 81. Visual north star

Coherence should feel like a quiet, inhabited place with history and intelligence behind it.

The environments may be beautiful and richly imagined, but the product should never ask the user to process that richness in order to act.

The functional experience remains spare.

Lumi supplies companionship.

Typography supplies clarity and character.

Materials supply continuity.

Space supplies metaphor.

The world supplies atmosphere.

And the interface keeps saying, in increasingly quiet ways:

You only need to look here right now.
