### CURRENT CANON

Focus Together exists now, but it lives on **Home**. A session carries the goal, first step, planned duration, elapsed-time bar and check-ins; meanwhile **Start with Lumi** on Today already hands the actual Right now intention and its first step into accompaniment, rather than asking the user to configure another workflow.

That works mechanically, but it blurs two spaces that the settled direction deliberately distinguishes. **Home is arrival, conversation, reflection and silence. Study is the narrowing of attention to present work.** Study is not implemented as a page or navigation destination, and Q2 leaves its access, its transition from Today, and the eventual placement of the session bar unresolved.

I also inspected the actual current **01-home** and **03-today-garden** captures for this run. The contrast is useful: Today already gives one visually dominant thing to do, while Home is intentionally broad and receptive.

### OPEN QUESTION

**When does “I’m going to do this” become Study—and does entering Study require the user to choose another mode?**

I think adding a permanent **Study** icon to the rail would solve the wrong problem. It would create another destination the user has to decide to visit, even though Coherence already knows when the user has crossed from considering work into actually beginning it.

At the other extreme, automatically throwing every task interaction into a focus room would be intrusive. The canon explicitly says not every beginning needs a formal session.

### PROPOSAL — **Study is a temporary focus state with an origin, not a permanent destination**

I’d make Study something Coherence enters **from the work the user has already chosen**, rather than somewhere they navigate to empty-handed.

The clearest transition is the existing **Start with Lumi** action on Today.

Today already says:

> This is the thing.  
> This is the first step.

So tapping **Start with Lumi** should remain exactly what its name promises: the start. No duration picker, no “What do you want to work on?”, no second confirmation.

But instead of carrying that work back into Home, Coherence transitions into **Study**.

The same shared intention comes with the user:

**Review Richard’s chapter reply**  
*First: open his comments and read once through.*

The current Focus Together component can move with it. If that action started a formal focus session, the elapsed-time/check-in bar appears inside Study, near the working interaction where it already conceptually belongs. The user has not configured anything new; Coherence has simply narrowed around the commitment they already made.

That makes the flow:

**Today → Start with Lumi → Study**

rather than:

**Today → Home → session machinery inside the arrival room**

Home still has an important route into Study, but only once conversation becomes concrete. **Just talk** should never suddenly become Study. **Break it down** can remain conversational while the person is figuring something out. **Body double**, however, naturally crosses the threshold once there is an actual thing to work on. Likewise Lumi might eventually say, *“Okay. We know what the first step is. Want to start?”* An affirmative answer can enter Study without another setup screen.

The important distinction is that **Study and Focus Together are not identical**.

Focus Together is an accompaniment strategy. Study is the cognitive state of narrowing around the present work.

A formal session may be active there, but Study should eventually also be able to hold untimed work. That prevents Coherence from teaching the user that every act of concentration requires a timer, check-in cadence or special productivity ritual.

I would also keep the existing navigation rail present rather than making Study a locked fullscreen mode. Orientation matters, and the user should always be able to leave. There simply would not need to be a permanent Study icon yet. Technically this could be a transient route/state entered with an origin such as Today or Home; exiting returns naturally to that origin.

And I’d carry forward the session-ending principle from the earlier exploration: **leaving Study does not mean the work failed**. If someone closes Coherence and keeps writing for an hour, the product succeeded. A timed session can stop being active without Coherence inventing an abandonment judgment.

This gives Study a very clean product role:

**Home helps me find the thread.**  
**Today helps me choose the thread.**  
**Study helps everything except the thread recede.**

That feels substantially different from adding another room to explore.

I’m deliberately not making a visual mockup on this run. The current screenshots were important for grounding the transition, but the open decision here is the **interaction architecture**. Drawing a new Study environment now would accidentally settle the room’s visual identity before deciding whether this “temporary state entered from chosen work” model is right. Once that is settled, the visual brief becomes much sharper: preserve the current rail and material language, carry forward the chosen intention and Lumi, and remove almost everything that asks the user to think about what comes next.
