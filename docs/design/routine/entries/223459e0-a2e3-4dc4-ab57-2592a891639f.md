## CURRENT CANON

I grounded this run in **COHERENCE_START_HERE**, then re-read **COHERENCE_CURRENT_STATE**, **COHERENCE_DESIGN_CANON**, **COHERENCE_OPEN_QUESTIONS**, and **COHERENCE_RECENT_CHANGES**.

Home is deliberately allowed to be a place for **arrival, reflection, conversation, and silence**. “Just talk” is already a first-class beginning, and the canon explicitly says reflection does not have to terminate in task creation. At the same time, Coherence is meant to help someone move when movement would actually serve them; Lumi should not become so deferential that every difficult loop receives endless reflective mirroring.

The unresolved tension is therefore not “reflection or productivity.” It is **how Lumi knows when the conversation has reached the point where action would help—and how she crosses that boundary without quietly deciding for the user.**

## DIRECTION

I think Coherence needs a recognizable **hinge between reflection and action**.

Not a mode selector.

Not “therapy mode” versus “productivity mode.”

And not a rule that every problem eventually becomes a task.

Instead:

> **Reflection continues until something changes in the conversation that makes choosing a direction useful. At that point Lumi surfaces the choice of whether to move—not the move itself.**

This would give Lumi permission to challenge gently while preserving the user’s authority over whether a reflective conversation becomes operational.

## OPEN QUESTION

This explores **Q23 — How should reflection and action balance?**

The current action-weighted prompting does not settle it. The canon says Home permits ambiguity and reflection without compulsory taskification, while Coherence’s broader purpose still includes beginning and meaningful action.

The question I’d narrow this to is:

> **What should happen at the moment Lumi senses that further talking may simply keep circling the same material?**

## PROPOSAL — **The Hinge**

Lumi should recognize a small number of conversational conditions that indicate a possible hinge:

- the user has arrived at a clear decision but has not acted on it;
- a concrete obligation has emerged from broader reflection;
- the conversation is repeating substantially without producing new understanding;
- the user explicitly asks what to do, how to start, or how to change something;
- Lumi sees a mismatch between the user’s stated intention and the pattern they are describing.

None of these should automatically create an Intention.

Instead, Lumi briefly makes the transition itself visible.

For example, after a long conversation about avoiding a practicum task:

> “I think we’ve understood the feeling pretty well. There may be a point here where more understanding won’t make starting easier. Do you want to stay with it a little longer, or should we find the smallest move?”

That is very different from:

> “Your next step is to open the document.”

The second answer silently decides that reflection is finished.

The first lets Lumi challenge without claiming that authority.

### The hinge has three legitimate exits

Conceptually, I think there are three outcomes, although I would **not** make these permanent UI buttons:

**Stay with it.**  
The user is not done thinking. Lumi continues without repeatedly trying to convert the conversation into action.

**Move with it.**  
The user wants the conversation to become operational. At this point Lumi has a bounded mandate to help identify a next move, reshape Today if appropriate, break something down, or start Focus Together.

**Leave it here.**  
Nothing else needs to happen.

That third outcome is unusually important.

A conversation might end with:

> “I think I just needed to realize that I don’t actually want this anymore.”

Coherence should be perfectly capable of responding:

> “Yeah. That feels like enough for tonight.”

No task.

No “Would you like me to make a plan?”

No dangling productivity obligation created from successful reflection.

### “Leave it here” does not have to mean “forget it”

This is where the earlier Thread exploration becomes useful.

Suppose the user has spent half an hour thinking through whether they want to remain in a particular career.

They do not want to act tonight.

But the conversation has produced a meaningful new orientation:

> The problem is no longer mainly burnout; the role itself no longer feels worth returning to.

That insight can update the relevant Thread or shared understanding **without becoming an Action**.

So reflection can have durable consequence without taskification.

This is exactly why the richer shared-life model matters: Coherence needs somewhere for **meaningful understanding** to go besides the to-do system.

## Lumi should challenge the loop, not the emotion

The behavioural distinction here matters.

A bad implementation would detect lengthy emotional conversation and eventually say:

> “Okay, but what are you going to do about it?”

That would make Lumi impatient with reflection.

The trigger should instead be a lack of **new movement in understanding**.

If the user is actively discovering something, Lumi stays with them.

If the conversation becomes:

> “I know.”  
> “I still can’t do it.”  
> “Yes, I know why.”  
> “I still can’t start.”

then a gentle hinge can be useful:

> “I’m not sure another explanation is what you’re missing here. Want to try approaching the beginning differently?”

The challenge is directed at the strategy, not at the person for still feeling something.

## Requests for action can skip the hinge entirely

The user should never have to go through this ritual when their intention is already clear.

> “Help me start my paper.”

No hinge required.

> “Can we just talk? I don’t want to solve this.”

That is equally clear. Lumi should not keep looking for an opening to operationalize it.

The hinge exists for **ambiguous transitions**, not as a mandatory conversational phase.

That fits the bounded-mandate work from previous explorations: explicit requests already establish scope.

## The hinge should become quieter as Lumi understands the person

Not because she learns that the user is “an action person” or “a reflection person.”

That would be too broad.

But explicit patterns can establish useful local preferences:

> “When I’m spiralling like this, it helps when you eventually challenge me.”

or:

> “If I say I just need to vent, please stop trying to fix it.”

Those are meaningful instructions.

Lumi can honour them without creating a personality-level model from one conversation.

## This also improves “Just talk”

Right now, **Just talk** risks being interpreted as a lane from which action is excluded, while other quick starts risk being interpreted as more productive or goal-directed.

I would avoid that.

“Just talk” should mean:

> **There is no assumed outcome.**

Sometimes that conversation remains reflection.

Sometimes it reveals an Intention.

Sometimes the user decides to let something go.

Sometimes the clearest outcome is:

> “I’m going to bed.”

All of those can be coherent conclusions.

## HISTORICAL / REJECTED

I would reject both extremes:

**Automatic taskification**  
Every meaningful conversation eventually produces a next step, reminder, intention, or plan.

and:

**Endless reflective accommodation**  
Lumi never risks suggesting that insight may have reached diminishing returns, because any movement toward action might feel pushy.

Both fail the same underlying test: they substitute a fixed assistant philosophy for what this particular moment requires.

I’d also avoid exposing **Reflect / Decide / Act** as persistent modes or tabs. The whole value of Lumi is that the user should not have to classify the kind of help they need before receiving it.

The principle I’d add to direction is:

> **Lumi does not force reflection toward action, but she is allowed to notice when reflection has reached a hinge. At that point she offers the transition itself for the user to choose. Meaningful reflection may update understanding without creating work.**

I’m not generating a mockup for this run. This proposal deliberately uses the **existing Home conversation surface** and should not introduce a new panel, mode selector, or persistent control; its quality depends on Lumi’s timing and language rather than a new visual component.
