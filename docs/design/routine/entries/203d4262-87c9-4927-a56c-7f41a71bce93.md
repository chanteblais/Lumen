### CURRENT CANON

Insights is currently a real, separate page that looks for **possible actions in recent Gmail**. A lead can become an intention through **Still needs doing**, or be resolved through **Let it go**. It is deliberately *not* an inbox: there are no unread counts, mail feed, refresh obligation, or assumption that every detected lead is a confirmed commitment.

At the same time, its long-term place in Coherence is explicitly unsettled. The design canon treats Home, Today, Library and eventual Study as cognitive projections of one shared life model, while **Insights’ eventual placement remains open**. The open-question document specifically asks whether these mail-derived leads should instead project through Library, Home, or somewhere else—without flooding Today or creating an inbox-processing ritual.

### OPEN QUESTION

**Should Insights actually be a place?**

The present implementation makes sense as a useful first container for the Gmail feature, but I think the separate destination risks giving tentative machine-detected obligations more psychological weight than they deserve.

A permanent nav item quietly says:

> “There is another place you should check.”

That sits awkwardly beside Coherence’s goal of reducing maintenance burden.

### PROPOSAL — **Insights is a review state, not a space**

I’d gradually turn Insights from a destination into a **temporary way Coherence brings uncertain outside-world information into view**.

The underlying mail-lead system can stay. What changes is the interaction model.

A Gmail-derived lead begins as something Lumi has **noticed**, not something the user owns.

For example:

> **I noticed something in your email about renewing your insurance. It sounds like it might need action.**

Then Coherence presents **one meaningful decision**:

**Still needs doing**  
**Already handled**  
**Not mine**  
**Let it go**

Only **Still needs doing** promotes the lead into the durable intention system.

Until then, it remains a tentative observation with provenance: *noticed from Gmail*, not *your task*.

That distinction is already implicit in the current Insights implementation; I’d make it the architectural boundary.

The more interesting part is **where these notices appear**.

I’d use two existing spaces differently.

**Home is where a lead may surface when it is genuinely relevant.** Not a panel of five email-derived tasks. Lumi can occasionally say something like:

> “One thing from your email may need a decision before tomorrow.”

The user can inspect it or simply keep talking. If they ignore it, Coherence does not create a guilt-producing pending review.

**Library is where noticed-but-unresolved material remains retrievable.** Once the Library navigation model exists, *Things Lumi noticed* could simply be one generated lens alongside other broad context. It is not a folder the user maintains and not a queue they are expected to clear.

Today stays protected.

**Tentative leads never enter Today.**

Today only sees something once the user—or sufficiently explicit conversation—has established that it is actually theirs and needs action.

That gives the whole flow a clean epistemic progression:

**Email → observed possibility → user decision → intention → Today if relevant**

rather than:

**Email → AI thinks task → productivity surface**

I would also preserve the current `/insights` page during the transition. It is implemented and useful. I would not remove the nav item until Home and Library provide genuinely equivalent access. Eventually, though, I think the best end state is that **Insights stops being one of Coherence’s “places.”**

That has a deeper payoff for future integrations.

Calendar, messages, documents and other connected systems will eventually produce the same kind of uncertain outside-world evidence. If each one becomes its own destination—Mail Insights, Calendar Insights, Drive Insights—Coherence turns into a collection of feeds.

Instead, they can all enter through the same conceptual layer:

> **Lumi noticed something that may matter.**

The source is provenance, not navigation.

That makes Insights less of a Gmail feature and more of an **epistemic boundary between the outside world and the user’s actual commitments**.

And that feels important to preserve: Coherence can look widely on the user’s behalf while remaining extremely conservative about what it declares to be part of their life.

I wouldn’t make a mockup for this exploration. The consequential choice is structural—**whether Insights is fundamentally a destination or a temporary review state**—and the existing visual system does not need a new aesthetic or shell to express it.
