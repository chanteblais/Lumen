### CURRENT CANON

Coherence already has the right conceptual shape for context: there is one shared life model, and the spaces are supposed to be **different projections of that same reality**, not separate databases. Home supports arrival and reflection, Today narrows toward present attention, Library exposes breadth and history, and Study is intended to narrow further toward the work immediately in front of you. The canon describes this as **life → evolving model → relevant context → today → now**. fileciteturn6file0L2-L2

The implementation has not caught up with that model yet. Lumi currently receives a **single capped context block** containing stored intentions, beliefs, recent activity, capacity, session and mail-lead information. Threads and the richer shared-life ontology are not yet first-class, and Q24 specifically leaves space-specific context, retrieval beyond the cap, relevance decay and Study compression unresolved. fileciteturn5file0L2-L2 fileciteturn7file0L2-L2

### OPEN QUESTION

How should Coherence decide **what Lumi needs to know right now** without either stuffing the entire user model into every conversation or deciding that information has stopped mattering simply because it is old?

This becomes increasingly important as the system gets richer. A six-month-old statement like *“finishing my degree matters more than maximizing income right now”* may be far more relevant to a practicum conversation than something the user happened to mention yesterday. Conversely, Lumi does not need every relationship, unfinished intention and past reflection loaded while helping someone write one paragraph.

The dangerous shortcut would be a generic relevance score with aggressive time decay. That would quietly recreate a familiar productivity-system assumption: **recent = relevant, inactive = unimportant**. The canon already rejects that logic. fileciteturn6file0L2-L2

### PROPOSAL — **Context is composed, not decayed**

I’d make Coherence’s context system work like a good editor assembling a briefing.

Nothing becomes less *true* merely because it falls out of the current brief. Instead, the system distinguishes between the **shared model** and the **working context selected from it**.

The shared model can remain broad. What Lumi actually receives for a turn is a much smaller, purpose-built **context brief**, assembled according to the space and the thing currently happening.

For example, Home’s brief might favour continuity: active life threads, explicit priorities, recent meaningful changes, unresolved conversations and anything the user has asked Lumi to keep in mind.

Today’s brief would favour present constraints: capacity, fixed commitments, scoped priority statements, current intentions, exclusions such as *not today*, and enough broader context to understand why one thing might matter more than another.

Study would become the most compressed. Once the user starts working on something, most of their life should effectively fall silent. Lumi might receive the current intention, the first meaningful step, relevant supporting material, known blockers, the user’s requested style of accompaniment and perhaps one or two pieces of context explaining why this work matters. She does **not** need the full backlog hovering behind every response.

Library would behave differently again. It is the place where breadth is welcome, so rather than loading everything into Lumi at once, it should support **retrieval**: the user or Lumi can reach back into dormant threads, relationships, older intentions and historical context as needed. That fits the canon’s insistence that Library be broader than a task list or archive without forcing the life model itself into literal shelves. fileciteturn6file0L2-L2

The architectural distinction I’d preserve is:

**Shared model → selected brief → conversation**

—not:

**memory → decay → deletion from context.**

That means time becomes only one signal among several. Explicit user statements, current relationships, active commitments, semantic connection to the present conversation, recent corrections and current-space relevance may all outweigh recency.

It also gives the earlier priority proposal somewhere clean to live. *“Practicum comes first this month”* does not have to appear in every prompt Lumi receives. It belongs in the shared model and gets pulled into Today, practicum conversations and relevant Study sessions because its **scope matches**, not because it sits permanently at the top of one giant memory block.

I would also keep one thin layer of observability. If Lumi draws on context the user might not expect, she should be able to answer:

> “Why did you bring that up?”

with something concrete:

> “Because you told me earlier this month that practicum was your main discretionary priority, and this decision affects your placement.”

That is much more useful than exposing a giant memory-management screen just to make the system technically transparent.

There is a subtle product principle here that I think belongs very deeply in Coherence:

**Absence from attention is not absence from the life model.**

Today can omit something without downgrading it. Study can forget the rest of your life for forty minutes without literally forgetting it. A relationship can go unmentioned for months and remain important. An old insight can suddenly become relevant again.

That separation is what lets Coherence truly **show less than it knows** without becoming unreliable. fileciteturn6file0L2-L2

I wouldn’t mock this one up. If it is working correctly, most of this architecture should be almost invisible. The user-facing effect is simply that Lumi seems to know **the right amount at the right moment**—and does not make the user continually re-teach her their life.
