### CURRENT CANON

I grounded this run in **COHERENCE_START_HERE**, then read **COHERENCE_CURRENT_STATE**, **COHERENCE_DESIGN_CANON**, **COHERENCE_OPEN_QUESTIONS**, and **COHERENCE_RECENT_CHANGES**.

Focus Together currently runs on Home with a planned duration, elapsed-time bar, and deterministic **15-minute check-ins**. When Lumi checks in, the user can answer **Yep, Stuck, Got distracted, or Done**. The canon is clear that Lumi should provide presence without unnecessary interruption, and an explicit instruction such as “don’t check on me unless I ask” is authoritative. Study must not become supervision.

The settled philosophy also matters here: Focus Together is not supposed to measure compliance. Leaving the app can be success, inactivity does not prove failure, and accompaniment should reduce the effort of staying with something rather than create another thing the user has to respond to.

### DIRECTION

I think body doubling in Coherence should feel less like **a timer periodically asking whether you are still behaving** and more like **another mind remaining available at the edge of attention**.

Sometimes that means silence. Sometimes it means a well-timed nudge. Sometimes the user genuinely wants Lumi to keep pulling them back.

The mistake would be treating all three as the same experience with a configurable interval.

### OPEN QUESTION

**Q18 — What are appropriate check-in defaults?**

The current fifteen-minute recurrence is understandable as an implementation starting point, but it makes elapsed time the reason Lumi speaks.

That conflicts slightly with a stronger existing principle:

> Lumi speaks when useful, not because engagement time elapsed.

The unresolved question, then, is not only *how often should Lumi check in?*

It is:

**What gives Lumi permission to re-enter the user’s attention during focused work?**

### PROPOSAL — **Make check-ins a session-level relationship, not a repeating timer**

I would give every Focus Together session an implicit **contact style**.

The user should usually not have to select that style from a setup screen. Lumi can infer it from how the session begins.

If the user says:

> “Stay with me while I finish this.”

that implies mostly **quiet presence**.

If they say:

> “Can you check on me so I don’t wander off?”

that explicitly asks for **active return points**.

If they choose **Body double** without saying anything else, I would use a middle default: **light touch**.

The important product behaviour is that Lumi briefly makes her interpretation legible when the session begins:

> “I’m here. I’ll let you work and check in once around the halfway point.”

or:

> “I’ll stay quiet unless you call me.”

or:

> “Okay. I’ll keep you company and give you a gentle nudge if we lose the thread.”

That one sentence creates a small social contract. The user knows what kind of presence they are getting without configuring a productivity mode.

From there, I would abandon the current **every N minutes indefinitely** model.

For ordinary light-touch sessions, there should usually be **one meaningful mid-session return point**, not a repeating sequence. A short session may need no mid-session interruption at all. A longer session might justify one return after enough time has passed for drift to become plausible. If the user explicitly asks for regular check-ins, then recurrence becomes appropriate because it is serving their stated need rather than a system default.

The existing responses become much more useful in this model.

If the user answers **Yep**, Lumi should generally **back off**:

> “Good. I’ll leave you with it.”

There is no reason that successful contact should automatically schedule another interruption fifteen minutes later.

If they answer **Stuck**, the session temporarily becomes collaborative. Lumi helps identify the obstruction, offers the smallest next threshold, and then recedes again once movement resumes.

If they answer **Got distracted**, Lumi should help with re-entry rather than treat the distraction as evidence of failure:

> “No problem. We were opening the intake form. Want to come back there?”

That may justify one nearer follow-up because the user has just demonstrated that additional contact is useful **in this session**.

And if the user simply ignores a check-in, Lumi should not escalate.

No second:

> “Still there?”

No notification because the first one was unanswered.

Silence is ambiguous. They may be concentrating. They may have walked away. They may be doing the work outside Coherence. The system does not know.

That restraint is important.

### A useful consequence: Lumi can adapt *within* a session without building a behavioural profile too quickly

Suppose someone repeatedly answers **Yep** immediately and continues working.

Lumi can become quieter during that session.

Suppose they twice say **Got distracted** and appreciate being brought back.

She can maintain a little more contact.

But this should not immediately become a permanent belief like:

> Chanté needs frequent supervision.

That would violate the existing distinction between contextual evidence and durable preference.

Across sessions, Lumi may eventually notice a pattern and offer it back:

> “You seem to do better when I check in once fairly early, then leave you alone. Want me to use that as the default?”

Now the user can explicitly authorize the preference.

That is a much cleaner learning loop than silently optimizing interruption frequency.

### The planned end should also change character

I would distinguish a **check-in** from the session’s planned ending.

A planned end is useful orientation:

> “The 45 minutes you set aside are up.”

But it should not imply:

> “Report your result.”

Lumi could simply surface:

> **Your planned time is up.**  
> Keep going · Come back to Lumi

If the user keeps working, Coherence does not need another timer decision immediately. If they come back, Lumi can help decide whether to continue, stop, capture where they got to, or simply leave.

This keeps Q18 separate from Q28: check-in behaviour does not need to solve the entire question of what a session ending means.

### Why I prefer this over three visible modes

I considered something like **Quiet / Light / Active body double**, but I would not expose those as permanent buttons unless later testing shows users need them.

That creates another piece of productivity vocabulary to understand and another decision before beginning.

Natural language already carries most of the signal:

> “Just sit with me.”  
> “Check on me in twenty minutes.”  
> “Keep me on track.”  
> “Don’t interrupt me unless I ask.”

Lumi should be good enough to understand those differences.

The interface only needs to make her interpretation visible enough to correct.

### HISTORICAL / REJECTED BOUNDARY

I would move away from fixed recurring fifteen-minute check-ins as the general default. They are useful implementation evidence, but I would not elevate them into the product philosophy.

I would also avoid gamified focus supervision, repeated unanswered nudges, “you’ve been inactive” messages, attention scores, or adapting permanent check-in frequency from a handful of observed sessions.

The principle I would add to the canon is:

> **Focused presence is quiet by default. Lumi re-enters attention when the user asked for contact, when the session established that contact is useful, or at a meaningful transition—not merely because another interval elapsed.**

I’m not generating a mockup for this exploration. The context package explicitly notes that **active Focus Together sessions are not pictured in the current visual-reference set**, so there is no current visual state I can honestly extend. This proposal is primarily behavioural; once an actual active-session capture is added, the small “what kind of presence to expect” cue would be worth exploring visually.
