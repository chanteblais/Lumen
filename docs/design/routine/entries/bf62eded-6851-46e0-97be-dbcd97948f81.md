## CURRENT CANON

I grounded this run in **COHERENCE_START_HERE**, then read **COHERENCE_CURRENT_STATE**, **COHERENCE_DESIGN_CANON**, **COHERENCE_OPEN_QUESTIONS**, and **COHERENCE_RECENT_CHANGES**.

Focus Together is currently implemented on Home as one session at a time, with a goal, first step, planned duration, elapsed-time bar, and deterministic check-ins. The current defaults are **45 minutes with 15-minute check-ins**, and the existing prompt offers **Yep / Stuck / Got distracted / Done**. Those timings are implementation, not settled product behaviour.

The stronger canon constraint is that Study/Focus must not become supervision. Lumi’s presence should help someone stay with something when useful, while inactivity is not evidence of failure and engagement time is not, by itself, a reason for Lumi to speak. The previous explorations also established useful boundaries: interruption requires its own authority, and session outcomes should not be inferred merely because someone stopped responding.

## DIRECTION

I think Focus Together should move away from a **fixed recurring check-in interval** and toward an **elastic cadence**.

The core principle would be:

> **Check in enough to restore the thread, then get quieter when the user appears to have it.**

That is subtly different from “personalize the timer.”

The point is not to discover whether a given person prefers 12-minute versus 17-minute intervals. The point is to make Lumi’s presence **responsive to what the user explicitly tells her during this session**, without requiring a setup flow or silently inferring concentration from inactivity.

## OPEN QUESTION

This directly explores **Q18 — What are appropriate check-in defaults?**

The current 15-minute recurrence works as an initial implementation, but it creates a structural risk: Focus Together can begin to feel like someone periodically asking whether you are still working.

That is especially odd once the support has succeeded.

If someone answers:

> “Yep.”

at 15 minutes, Lumi currently has evidence that they are still with the work. That should arguably make her **less** likely to interrupt them again soon, not simply restart the same countdown.

So the question I would resolve is:

> **Should Focus Together have a recurring check-in interval at all, or should the cadence respond to what actually happens during the session?**

## PROPOSAL — Elastic presence

I would keep the current experience extremely easy to start. **Start with Lumi still starts the session immediately.** No new “choose your focus style” screen, no cadence configuration, no timer wizard.

But underneath, the session begins with a default **light-presence cadence**.

For a typical 45-minute session, Lumi might schedule one first check-in around the current 15-minute mark. What happens after that depends on the user’s response.

If they choose **Yep**, that is positive evidence that no intervention is currently needed. Lumi should get quieter. Rather than automatically checking again another 15 minutes later, she could defer the next interruption until much nearer the planned end—or skip another check-in entirely.

So a perfectly ordinary 45-minute session might become:

> Start  
> 15 min — “Still with it?”  
> **Yep**  
> quiet  
> 45 min — planned-time transition

That already feels much less supervisory than three identical checks.

If the user chooses **Stuck**, the cadence changes because the user explicitly told Lumi that support is useful *now*. Lumi can briefly help unblock the task, then return them to it.

After that intervention, a somewhat earlier follow-up may actually make sense:

> “Want me to check back in a bit?”

If they accept, that creates a **session-scoped mandate** for closer presence.

If they choose **Got distracted**, Lumi can help them re-enter, but I would not interpret distraction as evidence that they permanently need more frequent monitoring. The next check-in can be somewhat nearer, but only within this session unless the user explicitly establishes a broader preference.

And if they choose **Done**, the session ends.

### Silence should also be a valid response

One important change: an unanswered check-in should **not escalate**.

Suppose “Still with it?” becomes available at 15 minutes and the user never answers because they are working in another window.

Coherence should not produce:

> “Just checking again…”

and then:

> “Are you still there?”

The unanswered check-in can simply remain pending until the user returns, or become irrelevant when the session reaches its planned end.

That follows directly from the canon:

> absence from Coherence is not evidence of disengagement from the work.

It also creates a nice asymmetry:

**explicit “I need help” → more presence**  
**explicit “I’m good” → less presence**  
**no information → do not assume**

### Check-ins should not automatically become external interruptions

This proposal also fits the initiation boundary from the previous exploration.

A Focus Together check-in can become available **inside the session UI** without necessarily creating an operating-system notification.

If the user remains inside Coherence, they see it.

If they switch to the document they are actually writing, Lumi does not automatically drag them back into Coherence every 15 minutes.

If the user explicitly says:

> “Please actually ping me every fifteen minutes or I’ll disappear.”

that is different. They have granted an initiation mandate, and a more active reminder pattern can be appropriate for that session.

This lets Focus Together support both kinds of people without a generic “notifications on/off” assumption.

## The planned end remains a separate event

I would keep the distinction from the session-ending exploration:

> **check-in cadence is about accompaniment; planned duration is about the container.**

Even if Lumi becomes quiet after one successful check-in, the planned duration still has meaning.

At 45 minutes:

> “That’s the time you set aside.”

with **Keep going / Wrap here / Done** still makes sense.

That is not Lumi checking whether the user behaved correctly. It is honouring the boundary they established when they began.

Someone can therefore have a session with only one actual Lumi interruption and still receive a meaningful closing transition.

## No hidden concentration inference

I would specifically resist making this smarter through signals like:

> no mouse movement → distracted  
> app hidden → working elsewhere  
> rapid typing → focused  
> long silence → flow state

Some of those may eventually become useful with explicit user consent, but they are unnecessary for the first good version of elastic presence.

The existing responses already provide high-quality, user-authorized signals.

**Yep** means: less help right now.

**Stuck** means: help with the obstacle.

**Got distracted** means: help me re-enter.

**Done** means: we can stop.

That is enough to make Focus Together feel substantially more intelligent.

## A standing preference can emerge only when explicitly stated

Over several sessions, the user might say:

> “You really don’t need to check on me once I get going.”

That can become durable preference:

> Lumi checks once near the beginning, then stays quiet unless asked.

Or:

> “For admin stuff, I actually need you to keep checking.”

That could become context-specific preference.

But five unanswered check-ins should not generate the inferred belief:

> user prefers fewer check-ins.

And three **Stuck** responses should not generate:

> user requires close supervision.

Behaviour within a session can control that session. Durable adaptation should require stronger evidence.

This keeps the model aligned with the explicit / observed / inferred distinctions already in canon.

## Why I prefer this over selectable modes

An obvious alternative would be a start screen with:

**Quiet company**  
**Occasional check-ins**  
**Keep me on track**

I would not start there.

Those modes sound useful, but they ask the user to diagnose what kind of support they need **before they have begun the thing they are struggling to begin**.

That is exactly the wrong moment to add another decision.

Elastic presence gets most of the benefit through the interaction they are already having.

If the user wants a different pattern, ordinary language remains available:

> “Stay close today.”

> “Don’t check on me unless I ask.”

> “Ping me in twenty minutes.”

No settings ceremony required.

## HISTORICAL / REJECTED

I would reject the idea that **15-minute check-ins should simply repeat until the timer ends** as the long-term design. It is a reasonable implementation default, but it treats accompaniment as scheduled polling rather than relational support.

I would also avoid progressively increasing check-ins because the user does not respond. Silence cannot tell Coherence whether they are distracted, deeply focused, away from the computer, or simply finished.

And I would avoid making cadence optimization into another behavioural scoring system. Focus Together does not need to learn a mathematically ideal interruption interval from completion rates.

The design principle I would add is:

> **Focus Together begins with light presence. Explicit need draws Lumi closer; explicit stability lets her recede; silence does not justify escalation. Check-ins adapt within the session before they become durable assumptions about the person.**
