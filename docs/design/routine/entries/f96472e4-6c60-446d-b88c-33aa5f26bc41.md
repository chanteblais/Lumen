## CURRENT CANON

I grounded this exploration in **COHERENCE_START_HERE**, then re-read **COHERENCE_CURRENT_STATE**, **COHERENCE_DESIGN_CANON**, **COHERENCE_OPEN_QUESTIONS**, and **COHERENCE_RECENT_CHANGES**.

Two existing constraints matter most here. First, Lumi should be present **without interruption**: engagement itself is never a reason to speak, and leaving the app can be a successful outcome. Second, Coherence already distinguishes between what it notices and what the user has actually adopted. The current Insights flow is a good example: a possible action found in email is tentative until the user confirms it.

The previous explorations add another useful distinction: **authority to act and authority to interrupt are not the same thing**. A standing mandate might allow Lumi to perform some operation once relevant context appears; it does not necessarily mean Lumi should break into the user’s attention to tell them about it.

## DIRECTION

I think Coherence needs a deliberate **initiation boundary**:

> **Lumi should notice broadly, surface selectively, and interrupt only under an explicit mandate.**

That gives Coherence three very different behaviours instead of one generic idea of “proactivity.”

Most information should be allowed to enter the shared model without demanding the user’s attention.

Some information deserves to be surfaced when the user is already present.

Very little should be allowed to reach out and interrupt them outside that context.

## OPEN QUESTION

This explores **Q9 — What may Lumi initiate?**

The open question currently covers reminders, notifications, and unsolicited context, while explicitly rejecting engagement as sufficient justification.

The key unresolved issue is:

> **When does something Lumi knows become something Lumi is entitled to place into the foreground?**

I think the answer should depend less on how “important” Lumi thinks the information is, and more on **which channel of attention she is entering**.

## PROPOSAL — **Three levels of initiation**

I would give Lumi three progressively stronger ways of bringing something forward.

### 1. Notice silently

This should be the default.

Lumi may discover:

- an email that appears relevant;
- a calendar change;
- a connection between two conversations;
- an emerging Thread;
- an upcoming deadline;
- a possible inconsistency in the shared model.

None of those facts automatically entitle her to speak.

They can simply become available context.

This is important because otherwise better context awareness paradoxically produces a noisier product. As Coherence learns more about someone’s life, it would interrupt them more often.

The opposite should happen:

> **Greater knowledge should allow better restraint.**

The earlier **Noticed** proposal fits directly here. External observations can exist in that tentative layer without becoming obligations or interruptions.

### 2. Surface in context

Lumi may bring something up **when the user is already in a relevant interaction** and the information materially changes what would otherwise happen.

This is the main form of useful proactivity I would encourage.

Suppose the user opens Today and asks:

> “What should I work on?”

Lumi knows that a meeting connected to the current Thread was moved forward by two days.

She can say:

> “One thing changed since we last looked at this: the supervision meeting is now Wednesday, so I’d put the case formulation ahead of the reading.”

She has initiated the *information*, but not the interaction itself.

Or the user returns to a Thread after a week and Lumi says:

> “There was a reply from Richard while this was in the background.”

Again, useful because the user is already standing at the relevant doorway.

This is where I would set the threshold:

> **Would withholding this information make the current interaction meaningfully worse or misleading?**

If no, Lumi can leave it alone.

That is stricter than:

> “Could this information be helpful?”

Almost everything could be helpful.

### 3. Interrupt

This is qualitatively different.

An operating-system notification, email, push alert, or unsolicited reminder reaches into attention the user has currently given to something else.

I would make the default rule very conservative:

> **Lumi does not interrupt outside Coherence unless the user has established a trigger that authorizes the interruption.**

Examples:

> “Remind me tomorrow morning to call the clinic.”

> “Tell me when Daniel replies.”

> “Let me know if that appointment changes.”

> “Don’t let me forget to submit this before Friday.”

Those are explicit initiation mandates.

Once they exist, Lumi does not need another permission step when the condition occurs. The notification is exactly what the user requested.

But something like:

> “This looks important.”

is not sufficient grounds for a push notification on its own.

That remains true even if Lumi is confident.

## This creates a useful distinction between **importance** and **interruptibility**

Something can matter enormously without warranting interruption.

For example:

> “You’ve been questioning whether this relationship is working.”

may be profoundly important context.

It would be bizarre for Lumi to push:

> **You haven’t resolved your relationship uncertainty.**

Likewise:

> “Your practicum is your main priority this semester.”

does not mean Lumi should periodically remind the user that practicum exists.

Importance primarily influences **interpretation and selection**.

Interruptibility is a separate property.

This is similar to the earlier priority proposal: urgency, importance, and current attention should not collapse into one scalar.

## Standing mandates can make Lumi feel much more capable without making her intrusive

The interesting opportunity is that initiation mandates do not have to be individual reminders.

The user might eventually say:

> “If a meeting changes on the same day, tell me.”

or:

> “If I’m about to miss something with a real deadline, I do want you to warn me.”

Those are broader standing mandates.

They define *what kind of interruption the user values*.

I would still keep them conversational rather than turning Settings into a giant matrix of notification permissions.

Lumi could remember:

> **Same-day calendar changes may interrupt.**  
> Explicitly requested by user.

> **Unconfirmed email actions should not interrupt.**  
> Default behaviour.

And if the user says:

> “Actually, stop warning me about calendar changes.”

that mandate disappears.

This fits the bounded-authority model from the previous exploration very cleanly.

## The mandate should include a natural scope

The user’s language often tells Coherence exactly how long an initiation rule lasts.

> “Remind me at 4.”

One event.

> “While I’m waiting to hear back, tell me if she emails.”

One Thread / temporary condition.

> “From now on, let me know if appointments change the same day.”

Standing rule.

> “This week, please keep me on top of my practicum deadlines.”

Temporary broader mandate.

Coherence does not need a separate notification-rule editor before it can understand those differences.

And once a temporary condition resolves, the initiation authority should resolve with it.

That prevents old requests from quietly becoming permanent nagging behaviour.

## In-app initiation should still respect **timing**

Even without an external notification, Lumi should not surface every relevant fact the moment the user opens Coherence.

Suppose Home loads and Lumi knows five things that changed.

The greeting should not become:

> “Three things happened while you were away…”

That recreates an inbox.

Instead, Coherence can retain those facts until they become relevant.

If one change genuinely alters the immediate landscape, it can earn the foreground:

> “Before we pick up where we left off, your Thursday appointment was cancelled.”

Everything else can remain available.

This preserves the existing principle:

> **show less than you know.**

## “Unsolicited help” should usually attach to a user move

I think there is a particularly elegant behavioural rule here:

> **Lumi may broaden the user’s current move, but should be cautious about creating a new move.**

If the user says:

> “I need to work on the practicum application.”

Lumi can initiate relevant context:

> “The deadline is next Monday, and you already drafted the experience section.”

That improves the move they already made.

If the user is simply sitting on Home doing nothing, those same facts do not automatically justify:

> “You should work on your practicum application.”

That would create a new demand.

This boundary would make Lumi feel perceptive rather than managerial.

## Urgent safety or hard external consequences need their own explicit policy

I would not hide this edge case inside a general “Lumi can interrupt when important enough” rule.

Certain future integrations could involve genuinely consequential events: a meeting being cancelled shortly before departure, a flight change, perhaps financial or health-related information.

Those domains may justify different initiation policies.

But they should be decided **per capability**, with clear user expectations, rather than smuggling a universal emergency exception into Lumi’s personality.

That keeps Q9 tractable.

## Relationship to earlier explorations

This gives several previous ideas a clean place in the system.

**Noticed** answers:

> What has Lumi observed that the user has not adopted?

**Bounded mandates** answer:

> What may Lumi do with authority?

This proposal answers:

> When may Lumi claim the user’s attention?

Those are three different questions.

A noticed email might be stored silently.

A user mandate might allow Lumi to convert confirmed facts into Today without repeated approval.

But only an initiation mandate would allow Lumi to push a notification about it.

Separating those layers makes the system much easier to reason about.

## HISTORICAL / REJECTED

I would reject **engagement-driven proactivity** entirely:

- “You haven’t opened Coherence today.”
- “You seem inactive.”
- “Want to keep your momentum going?”
- “It’s been three hours since your last task.”
- notifications generated because Lumi has something interesting to say.

I would also reject a single global setting like **Lumi proactivity: Low / Medium / High**. It is too blunt. A user may want aggressive calendar alerts and absolutely no productivity nudges.

And I would avoid treating machine confidence as permission:

> “Lumi is 95% confident this matters, therefore notify.”

Confidence can influence whether a claim is surfaced; it cannot grant access to attention.

The design principle I would add is:

> **Lumi notices broadly, surfaces context when it improves an interaction already underway, and interrupts only within an explicit initiation mandate. Importance alone does not create interruptibility.**

I’m not generating a mockup for this exploration. The unresolved decision is primarily a **behaviour and attention-policy boundary**, and inventing a notification-centre or settings surface would prematurely turn that policy into a new interface.
