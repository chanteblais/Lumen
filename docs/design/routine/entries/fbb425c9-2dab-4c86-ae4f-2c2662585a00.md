### CURRENT CANON

The current Library is genuinely just the room: the actual screenshot shows the open central reading circle/table, surrounding shelves, blank plaques, stairs, and Lumi—but none of those are currently interactive. The old grouped list panel was intentionally removed, while the underlying lists still exist and continue feeding Lumi and Today. fileciteturn12file0L2-L2

The settled direction is broader than “lists with prettier scenery”: Library should expose active context, relationships, history and developing ideas without forcing the life model into a shelf hierarchy or making organization depend on filing. Lists must remain accessible, but they should not become the psychological centre of the app. fileciteturn13file0L2-L2

### OPEN QUESTION

**What is the Library’s primary interaction?**

I think this needs to be settled before deciding which shelves, plaques or alcoves are clickable.

The current room tempts a literal solution—School goes on this shelf, relationships go there, ideas live upstairs—but that would quietly turn the beautiful environment into a filing system the user has to understand and maintain.

### PROPOSAL — **Bring things to the reading table**

I’d make the **central reading circle the Library’s one obvious point of entry**.

Not every shelf. Not four permanent categories on the plaques. Not a task panel laid back over the room.

The mental model becomes:

> **The Library contains everything. The table is where you ask to see something.**

Selecting the table opens a restrained parchment **Index** anchored to that central space. The Index is primarily retrieval, not organization.

Its first level could be extremely small:

**Find something**  
Search in ordinary language: *“that thing I was thinking about attachment last month,” “what am I still doing for school?”, “Daniel,” “tax stuff.”*

**Browse what’s here**  
A few generated lenses into the shared model: active threads, people/relationships, commitments, ideas and useful history. These are *views*, not storage locations.

**My lists**  
School, Work, Personal and Later remain directly available for the moments when someone really does want the familiar list representation.

That last part matters. I wouldn’t hide Lists in the name of conceptual purity. They are useful existing structure. I’d simply make them **one lens into the Library rather than the architecture of the Library itself**.

When something is chosen, I’d bring its contents **to the table** rather than navigating to a literal bookshelf. A thread, relationship, list or old idea could open as a folio/book-like reading surface in the central circle, with the room remaining visibly behind it.

That solves several problems at once.

It preserves the spatial feeling without asserting that digital objects literally live in painted shelves. It gives an overwhelmed user one clear affordance instead of turning every object in the environment into something they need to test. And it gives future concepts—Threads, relationships, evolving ideas—somewhere to appear without requiring a complete ontology before Library becomes usable.

It also makes the blank plaques interesting in a different way. I would **not** assign them permanent categories yet. They could remain environmental until the interaction model proves it needs them. Later they might become contextual cues, but there is no reason to commit them to `School / Work / Personal / Later` now and recreate the old Lists page spatially.

The principle I’d carry forward is:

**Library is organized by retrieval, not by filing.**

Coherence should know where things belong conceptually so the user doesn’t have to.

And the current Library image actually supports this unusually well: the room already has a strong central clearing that visually reads as a place where material can be *brought forward*, while the surrounding shelves communicate enormous breadth without needing to become a wall of controls.

I inspected the actual current Library capture for this run, but I’d hold off on a mockup for one more step. The consequential choice here is whether **“bring it to the table”** is the right interaction metaphor. Once that is accepted, the Index itself is concrete enough to design rather than producing another attractive Library mockup that accidentally settles the wrong architecture.
