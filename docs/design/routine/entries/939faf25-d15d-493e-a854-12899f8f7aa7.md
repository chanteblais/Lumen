### CURRENT CANON

Home is deliberately the place where someone can arrive **before they know what they need**. It already supports reflection, retrieval, messy brain-dumps, orientation and action, and the design explicitly says that clarity itself can be a successful outcome; conversation should not reflexively become a task, plan or deadline. fileciteturn12file0L2-L2

### OPEN QUESTION

How does Lumi know whether this particular conversation needs **company, clarity, or movement**?

Right now those possibilities are all present philosophically, but there isn't yet a clear behavioural rule governing when Lumi should stay with an experience, help make sense of it, or begin converting it into action.

### PROPOSAL — **An invisible conversational stance**

I think Lumi should maintain a lightweight, temporary sense of the **kind of help this conversation currently needs**.

Not a mode the user chooses. Not a personality setting. Not another piece of permanent memory.

Just an internal conversational stance that can move naturally between three states:

**Hold** — stay with the thought.  
**Orient** — help the user understand what is happening or decide what matters.  
**Move** — help something actually begin.

The interesting part is that Coherence already almost has the vocabulary for this.

`Just talk` is essentially **Hold**.

`Help me choose` is **Orient**.

`Break it down` and `Body double` are different expressions of **Move**.

So rather than adding a feature, I'd treat the existing quick starts as explicit training signals for a deeper behaviour that also works in ordinary conversation.

If someone opens Home and writes:

> “I don't know if counselling is actually what I want anymore.”

Lumi should probably begin in **Hold**. She can explore the contradiction, retrieve relevant context and help the person hear what they're saying. No “Would you like me to make a plan?” at the end.

If that conversation becomes:

> “I think what's actually bothering me is the practicum placement, not counselling.”

the stance may naturally become **Orient**. Lumi helps separate those things and reconstruct what seems true.

And if it becomes:

> “Okay. I need to email Richard and figure out my options.”

the conversation has crossed into **Move**. Now breaking down the email, creating an intention, or starting together makes sense.

The stance should be **ephemeral and fluid**. Lumi shouldn't learn “Chanté prefers reflection” because one conversation stayed reflective. One conversation might move Hold → Orient → Move; another might end entirely in Hold. That ending is not unfinished.

The most useful rule may be:

**Lumi should not operationalize a thought until the conversation shows evidence that the user wants the thought to become movement.**

Evidence can be explicit — *“help me do this,” “what should I do?”, “I've decided…”* — or strongly implied by the interaction. When Lumi genuinely cannot tell, she can occasionally ask something lightweight like:

> “Do you want to stay with this for a bit, or figure out what to do with it?”

But that should be an uncertainty repair, not a ritual that appears at the end of every reflective conversation.

This also gives the model a cleaner behavioural target. Instead of implicitly rewarding responses that end in a tool call or action, evaluation can ask whether Lumi selected the **appropriate outcome for the user's current stance**. Sometimes the best result is an intention created. Sometimes it is a clearer decision. Sometimes it is simply, *“Yes. That's the thing that's actually bothering me.”*

I wouldn't make a visual mockup for this one. Making the stance visible would probably weaken the idea by turning Home into a three-mode interface. Its value is precisely that **the user can continue speaking normally while Lumi gets better at understanding what kind of help is appropriate.**

And I think it connects unusually well to Coherence's central inversion: **the system does more interpretive work so the user has to do less interface work.**
