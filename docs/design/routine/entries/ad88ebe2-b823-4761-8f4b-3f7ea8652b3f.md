## CURRENT CANON

I grounded this run in **COHERENCE_START_HERE**, then read **COHERENCE_CURRENT_STATE**, **COHERENCE_DESIGN_CANON**, **COHERENCE_OPEN_QUESTIONS**, and **COHERENCE_RECENT_CHANGES**.

Coherence already has an important distinction in its underlying philosophy: explicit, observed, inferred, and derived information are not the same thing. A guess must not overwrite something the user actually said; history can remain true as history without continuing to act as present truth. Lumi currently receives stored intentions, beliefs, recent activity, capacity, session context, and mail-lead context, and beliefs already carry source/confidence information internally. At the same time, **Settings is currently only a placeholder** for things like name, timezone, and “what Lumi knows,” while Library is directionally meant to expose the broader shape of the user’s life without becoming the database itself.

The settled constraint here is also strong: transparency and correction matter, but **the user should not have to curate memory as a recurring chore**.

## DIRECTION

I think the eventual memory surface should answer a different question from most AI “memory managers.”

Not:

> **What does Coherence have stored about me?**

but:

> **What is currently shaping how Lumi is helping me?**

That distinction could prevent a fairly nasty product trap.

If Coherence eventually knows hundreds or thousands of things about someone, exposing all of them in a management interface simply transfers the maintenance burden back to the user. They start reviewing, pruning, categorizing, deciding what deserves permanence, wondering whether some forgotten statement is influencing the system.

The useful transparency boundary is **influence**, not storage.

## OPEN QUESTION

This is **Q12 — How can users inspect/correct memory?**

Q19 and Q25 constrain it, but I would leave those questions separately unresolved: consequential inference still needs its own rules around provenance and confirmation, and retention/deletion still needs an actual policy.

The narrower question for this exploration is:

> **How can a person understand and correct Lumi’s working understanding without becoming the administrator of Lumi’s memory?**

## PROPOSAL — **“What’s shaping this?” rather than a memory database**

I would make memory inspection primarily **contextual**.

Whenever Lumi makes a recommendation that meaningfully depends on remembered context, the user should have an unobtrusive way to ask:

> **Why are you thinking that?**

Not necessarily a literal button on every message. Conversation itself can do most of the work:

> “Why did you put practicum first?”

Lumi might answer:

> “Mostly because you told me practicum is your main priority this semester, and you have a client session tomorrow. I’m also treating your energy today as fairly low, so I kept the rest smaller.”

That explanation is already more useful than showing a database row.

But occasionally the user will want to inspect the underlying context more directly. I’d give consequential explanations a quiet **What’s shaping this?** affordance that opens a compact view of the few pieces of context actually involved.

For that practicum recommendation, it might show:

> **Practicum is your main priority this semester**  
> You told Lumi this · Aug 14  
> Used to help choose what gets protected when several things compete.

> **Your client session is tomorrow**  
> Calendar / current commitment  
> Used because it affects timing.

> **Today feels low-capacity**  
> You told Lumi this this morning  
> Used only for today.

The important thing is what is *not* shown: every related memory, every old conversation, confidence percentages, internal embedding matches, or a giant graph of the user’s life.

Coherence exposes the **reasoning-relevant slice**.

### Correction should happen at the same point as inspection

Each piece of context should be correctable in ordinary language.

If the user sees:

> **Practicum is your main priority this semester**

they might say:

> “That’s not really true anymore.”

Lumi can respond:

> “Got it. I won’t treat practicum as your main semester priority anymore. It still matters, but I’ll stop giving it that special weight.”

That is a far better correction experience than opening a memory-management page, locating a record, editing its value, choosing whether to archive it, and saving.

And importantly, **the old statement does not have to become false**. It can remain part of history:

> On Aug 14, the user said practicum was their main priority.

What changes is whether that statement continues to shape present recommendations.

That preserves the canon’s distinction between history, supersession, and current truth.

## A central surface can still exist — but it should show active influence

Settings currently promises something around “what Lumi knows.” I would eventually narrow that to something like:

> **What Lumi is using**

This would not be the canonical store of all memory.

It would show only context that currently has meaningful behavioural influence: enduring priorities, standing preferences, important relationships or commitments, and perhaps a small number of active inferences Lumi is relying on.

If there are 800 stored facts but only 11 materially affect current behaviour, the surface shows roughly those 11.

That immediately makes the page more comprehensible.

It also answers a much more psychologically relevant concern:

> “What assumptions is this system making about me right now?”

rather than:

> “Can I audit every byte the system has ever retained?”

The latter belongs to privacy/export controls under Q25, not the everyday product experience.

## Inferences deserve slightly different treatment

Explicit user statements and Lumi’s inferences should not look identical.

Suppose Lumi has begun to infer:

> **Mornings have been harder lately.**

If that begins affecting Today, the inspection should make its status clear:

> **Mornings may be lower-capacity lately**  
> Lumi’s impression from recent conversations  
> Used lightly when suggesting when to begin demanding work.

And the correction should be easy:

> “That’s not really a pattern.”

Lumi drops the inference.

Or:

> “Yes, but only on practicum days.”

Now the user has supplied better scope.

This is exactly the kind of moment where correction should **improve the model**, rather than merely delete an offending sentence.

## “Don’t use this” should be distinct from “delete this”

I think this distinction will matter a lot.

A user might reasonably say:

> “I know I told you my relationship was rocky in July, but don’t use that when you’re helping me now.”

That does not necessarily mean:

> erase every historical reference to July.

So the ordinary correction vocabulary should include a concept like:

> **Stop using this to guide me**

without pretending that this is equivalent to deletion.

Actual deletion, export, retention, and propagation belong to Q25 and need stronger guarantees before the interface promises anything.

This proposal therefore gives users practical agency **without prematurely inventing the privacy semantics**.

## The system should surface memory only when there is something consequential to correct

I would not periodically ask:

> “Review what Lumi knows?”

No weekly memory hygiene.

No badge saying **6 memories need review**.

No “Improve Lumi’s understanding” checklist.

That would create exactly the administrative burden Coherence is meant to absorb.

Instead, transparency becomes **proportional to consequence**.

If a piece of remembered context is merely helping Lumi understand a casual conversation, it does not need a visible provenance marker.

If it materially changes Today, causes Lumi to challenge the user, affects a standing recommendation, or expands what Lumi is willing to do autonomously, then its provenance becomes more important.

That gives the product a clean principle:

> **The more a remembered belief affects the user’s experience, the easier it should be to inspect and correct.**

## Why this fits Library as well

I would keep Library primarily about the **shape of life**, not the mechanics of memory.

The user may eventually explore a Thread, relationship, project, or period of history there and encounter remembered context naturally.

But Library should not become:

> “Here are 347 facts Lumi knows.”

That would confuse the representation of the user’s life with the machinery used to understand it.

Settings can hold the transparency/control surface. Library can hold meaningful context as content. Conversation remains the easiest correction mechanism.

Those roles stay distinct.

## HISTORICAL / REJECTED

I would avoid a conventional AI memory manager containing a long flat list of remembered facts with Edit / Delete toggles. It looks transparent but creates a maintenance obligation and exposes implementation structure rather than useful meaning.

I would also avoid confidence percentages such as **72% confident** in the normal interface. “Lumi’s impression,” “you told me,” “from your calendar,” and “based on recent conversations” are generally more legible forms of epistemic transparency.

And I would not treat **forget**, **stop using**, **archive**, **supersede**, and **delete** as interchangeable operations. The canon already points toward those distinctions; the interface should not collapse them just because a trash icon is easy to build.

The principle I’d add to the design direction is:

> **Memory transparency should follow influence. Users need to understand and correct the context shaping Lumi’s behaviour, not maintain an inventory of everything Coherence has ever retained.**

I’m not generating a mockup on this run. The central decision is the **inspection model and correction boundary**; until Q25 establishes what deletion and retention actually mean, a polished “memory management” screen could accidentally imply guarantees the product has not yet settled.
