### CURRENT CANON

Coherence already has the pieces for a useful autonomy model, but not yet a settled policy. Lumi can create, update, complete, reopen and drop intentions, reshape Today after conversational input, and surface quiet ledger lines such as **Noted**, **Done**, **Let go**, and **Reshaped Today**. At the same time, the canon says **“automate administration, never agency”** and that explicit user instructions retain authority within their scope. fileciteturn4file0L2-L2 fileciteturn5file0L2-L2

The unresolved Q10 is exactly where the boundary belongs: confirmation should depend on **uncertainty, consequence and reversibility**, but the exact boundary between interpretation, proposal and committed action is still open. fileciteturn6file0L2-L2

### OPEN QUESTION

How can Lumi become genuinely useful enough to *do things for you* without turning every interaction into either:

- “Would you like me to…?” confirmation fatigue, or
- an AI quietly rewriting your life model behind your back?

### PROPOSAL — **Lumi may tidy the foreground; she may not rewrite your life**

I’d make that the governing rule for autonomy.

Lumi gets progressively more authority based on the **scope of the consequence**, rather than by feature.

**1. Foreground actions — just do them.**  
These alter what is being shown or attended to *right now*, without changing the durable meaning of the user’s life.

For example, after “I’m exhausted today,” Lumi could shrink Today, move the demanding item out of Right now, and surface an easier step without asking permission for every rearrangement. That behaviour is already conceptually consistent with Today adapting to capacity and conversational reshape. fileciteturn4file0L2-L2

**2. Reversible organizational actions — do them, then make the action legible.**  
If Lumi changes something persistent but easily reversible, she can act and leave a quiet ledger line:

**Reshaped Today · Undo**  
**Moved back to Work · Undo**  
**Marked complete · Undo**

The existing ledger language gives Coherence a natural mechanism for this. It means the user doesn’t have to approve routine bookkeeping, but nothing mysterious happens either. fileciteturn4file0L2-L2

**3. Meaning-changing actions — confirm first.**  
Anything that changes what something *means* should require explicit authority: abandoning an intention, changing an enduring priority, deciding that an old commitment no longer matters, converting an inference into something treated as known, or changing a commitment’s actual date.

That distinction matters because Coherence explicitly separates importance, urgency, present relevance, archive, supersession and deletion. fileciteturn5file0L2-L2

**4. External or destructive actions — explicit command only.**  
Sending something, deleting something irrecoverable, cancelling something, or acting in another person’s direction should never emerge merely because Lumi inferred it was sensible.

The nice consequence is that Lumi could feel **far less timid** without becoming controlling.

You could say:

> “School is frying my brain today.”

…and Lumi could immediately make Today gentler.

But she would not silently conclude:

> *School is no longer important to Chanté.*

That becomes the central distinction:

**Lumi is allowed to interpret the present aggressively; she should alter the enduring model conservatively.**

There is also an implementation advantage. Every tool action could carry three small pieces of policy metadata:

**scope** — foreground / persistent / external  
**reversibility** — easy / costly / irreversible  
**authority** — explicit / strongly implied / inferred

Those three values can determine whether Coherence **acts**, **acts + offers Undo**, or **asks first**. This turns a fuzzy personality question into a reusable system rule across Today, Library, memory, Study, mail, and future integrations.

I wouldn’t make a mockup for this one. The important artefact is the behavioural rule; visually it should mostly reuse Coherence’s existing quiet ledger interactions rather than introduce another UI surface.

This feels like a particularly strong fit for the product because it gives Lumi more actual usefulness while preserving the thing Coherence cares about most: **administrative burden can disappear without the user surrendering authorship.**
