### CURRENT CANON

I grounded this run in **COHERENCE_START_HERE**, then read **COHERENCE_CURRENT_STATE**, **COHERENCE_DESIGN_CANON**, **COHERENCE_OPEN_QUESTIONS**, and **COHERENCE_RECENT_CHANGES**.

The progression boundary is already unusually clear. Coherence should **not** have completion currency, an earn/spend economy, unlock thresholds, a storefront built around productivity rewards, mandatory care, streak loss, or visible punishment for absence. Environmental growth is allowed, but its purpose is **continuity and familiarity**, not proving that the user has been productive.

That means a hard month cannot make the world look neglected or underdeveloped. Starting, returning, reflection, changing direction, letting go, and completing are all potentially meaningful; completion cannot secretly become the score everything else is measured against.

The open territory is what environmental continuity actually responds to and what it means.

### DIRECTION

I think the strongest interpretation is:

> **The world should change because Coherence knows you better, not because you performed better.**

That is a much more distinctive progression system than simply making traditional gamification gentler.

### OPEN QUESTION

**Q6–8 — What does environmental continuity change, where, and how?**

The obvious implementation is some hidden accumulation system:

use Coherence → points invisibly rise → room gains objects.

I would avoid that even if the points are never shown. It is still a productivity score wearing scenery.

Instead, I’d base progression on **representational maturity**.

Something changes in the environment when part of the user’s life becomes meaningfully *known*.

### PROPOSAL — **Traces of continuity**

Coherence gradually acquires small persistent environmental **traces** of the life it has accompanied.

A trace is not earned.

It is not a reward for completing something.

It appears when some piece of context has developed enough continuity that it is no longer just a passing datum.

For example, imagine someone begins talking to Lumi about changing careers.

At first, that is simply conversation.

Over the following months it appears again: uncertainty, possible training, financial constraints, an application, a setback, renewed interest.

Eventually Coherence recognizes:

> This is not a stray thought. This has become part of the shape of this person’s life.

That is when the environment may quietly acquire a persistent trace.

Not:

> **Career Change — Level 3 unlocked!**

More like an object or visual detail that makes the space feel increasingly inhabited and particular.

The exact asset language should remain a later art decision. The important product rule is what the object **means**:

> **Something has become part of our shared continuity.**

A difficult unresolved Thread can create a trace.

A relationship can.

A recurring creative interest can.

A period of rest can.

Something the user ultimately abandons can.

A finished project can.

None is intrinsically more valuable than the others.

### Traces should not disappear when the user disappears

This is probably the most important rule.

If someone does not open Coherence for six weeks, their environment should look exactly as welcoming when they return.

No wilted plants.

No extinguished hearth.

No dusty room signalling neglect.

No companion commenting on absence through scenery.

Continuity accumulates; absence does not subtract.

That makes return psychologically cheap.

### The environment should become more *specific*, not necessarily more crowded

I also would not equate progression with continually adding objects.

That eventually produces visual entropy and subtly creates a completion collection game.

Instead, traces can deepen existing details.

A space might gradually acquire:

- a more particular object among existing scenery,
- a small inscription or material change,
- a familiar arrangement that persists,
- a subtle seasonal or lighting variant associated with shared history,
- one replacement detail rather than one additional detail.

The mature state should feel **more yours**, not more full.

That distinction matters for Coherence’s existing visual quiet.

### Some traces could be tied to Threads — but never require Threads

There is a nice future connection to the richer shared model.

If a persistent Thread eventually exists as first-class structure, it could sometimes provide the semantic anchor for a trace.

But the user should not have to formally create or name a Thread to qualify.

The system can notice continuity before ontology catches up.

Likewise, removing or superseding something in the life model does not necessarily mean destroying the environmental trace. The history still happened.

That gives the world something conventional productivity software almost never represents:

**past significance without current obligation.**

A project can be over and still have mattered.

### Lumi should very rarely explain progression unprompted

I would resist celebratory pop-ups such as:

> “Your room has grown!”

That immediately turns the change into a reward event.

Ideally the user simply notices:

> “Wait—is that new?”

Then Lumi can answer:

> “A little. That part of your life has been around with us for a while.”

That is much more intimate than:

> “You unlocked a new object.”

It makes the environmental change feel like recognition rather than reinforcement.

There could eventually be an accessibility-friendly way to review recent environmental changes, but it should not become a collectibles catalogue by default.

### This creates progression without a hidden performance metric

The trigger is not:

**How much did you do?**

It is closer to:

**Has something acquired enough continuity or meaning that the shared environment can honestly reflect it?**

That still requires implementation criteria, but those criteria can be plural rather than scalar.

A candidate trace might arise because something has:

- persisted across meaningfully separated moments,
- been explicitly described as important,
- accumulated history or revision,
- been returned to after an interruption,
- changed form significantly while remaining recognizably the same context.

Those are **signals of continuity**, not points.

Lumi could propose a trace only when several forms of evidence agree, avoiding an invisible `continuity_score = 73`.

### There should also be room for explicit authorship

Occasionally the user may simply want something represented.

> “This year changed my life. I want something in here that remembers it.”

That should be enough.

Explicit meaning should outrank algorithmic eligibility.

This fits the broader Coherence principle that inference assists but does not overrule agency.

### Why I think this solves the original progression desire better than rewards

A reward system says:

> I did things, therefore I obtained things.

This system says:

> I lived something, therefore this place remembers it.

That makes the environment a visible consequence of **relationship and history**, rather than an incentive structure wrapped around task completion.

It also gives someone who is struggling just as much potential for a rich, deeply personalized world as someone having an extremely productive year.

That is a pretty important test of whether the progression system actually belongs in Coherence.

### HISTORICAL / REJECTED

I would continue to reject **Coherence points**, completion flowers, spendable balances, unlock trees, streak-based growth, shop inventories purchased through productivity, environmental decay, and “care for Lumi/the room by getting things done.”

I would also reject a hidden universal growth score even if users never see it. That preserves the rejected premise while merely hiding the accounting.

The canon-level principle I’d propose is:

> **Environmental progression reflects accumulated relationship with the user’s life, not accumulated performance. Coherence becomes more particular as shared context becomes more meaningful; absence never makes it less whole.**

I’m not generating a mockup on this run. The key decision is the **meaning and trigger model** for environmental change. Settling that first should make later visual exploration much safer; otherwise almost any charming progression mockup risks accidentally designing a reward system before we have defined what the world is actually responding to.
