## Purpose

This document defines the philosophy, interaction model, AI behaviour, information flow, and environmental role of Focus, represented within Coherence as the Study.

The Study is where intention becomes attention.

The Garden asks:\
What deserves tending today?

The Study asks:\
What are we attending to now?

Its purpose is not to measure how long the user can remain productive. It is to reduce the world to one meaningful thing, help the user cross the activation threshold when necessary, provide the useful presence of Lumi, preserve context through interruption, and then get out of the way when momentum exists.

The Study should embody one of Coherence’s deepest forms of compression:

life → relevant context → Today → this one thing

## 1. The Study is an attention environment

Focus should not be conceived primarily as a timer feature.

The Study is an environment designed around a cognitive state: sustained or intentional attention to one thing.

The object of attention may be:\
- an Action\
- part of an Intention\
- a decision\
- a piece of creative work\
- reading\
- administrative work\
- a conversation the user needs to prepare for\
- something vague that first needs orientation

The defining property is not task type. It is that the user has chosen, or is trying to choose, to stay with something for now.

## 2. One thing should dominate

When the user enters the Study, competing context should recede.

The Study should generally foreground:\
- what we are working on\
- the immediate next action, when useful\
- Lumi\
- minimal controls\
- an optional sense of time

It should not foreground:\
- the rest of Today\
- overdue work\
- other Lists\
- analytics\
- notifications\
- unrelated calendar items\
- motivational copy

The Study is the narrowest attention surface in Coherence.

## 3. Entering the Study should feel like crossing a threshold

The transition from Garden to Study should communicate:\
We have chosen. Now we attend.

This can be achieved through visual and spatial simplification rather than dramatic animation.

The environment may become quieter. Other information may recede. Lumi may settle into a different posture or location.

The user should feel less need to continue deciding.

## 4. Focus can begin from anywhere

The Study should not require the user to follow a rigid Garden → Study workflow.

The user may begin Focus from:\
- Today / Garden\
- Lists / Library\
- Home conversation\
- a direct command to Lumi\
- a specific Thread or Action

Examples:\
“Stay with me while I answer this email.”\
“Let’s work on the paper.”\
“I need to do this for half an hour.”

Coherence should carry the relevant context into the Study automatically.

## 5. Starting and focusing are related but distinct

Sometimes the user has already begun and simply wants company while continuing.

Sometimes the primary difficulty is crossing the activation threshold.

The Study should support both.

Lumi should determine whether the user needs:\
- clarification\
- a smaller first action\
- task decomposition\
- quiet body doubling\
- a timer\
- help reconstructing context\
- nothing except permission to begin

Do not automatically turn every Focus session into a planning conversation.

## 6. The first action may be deliberately tiny

When initiation is the problem, Lumi should be comfortable proposing something smaller than conventional productivity advice would consider meaningful.

For example:\
User: I know I need to write this paper. I just can’t start.\
Lumi: Is the document open?\
User: No.\
Lumi: Open it. I’ll wait.

Then:\
Read the last paragraph you wrote.

The purpose is not infantilization.

It is to cross the smallest meaningful activation threshold.

Once momentum appears, Lumi should stop shrinking the task and let the user work.

## 7. Lumi should diagnose the friction lightly

“I can’t focus” can mean different things:\
- I do not know what comes next\
- the task is too large\
- I have lost context\
- I am anxious about doing it badly\
- I am bored\
- I am exhausted\
- I am distracted\
- something else feels more important\
- I need another presence

Lumi should become good at distinguishing these states without interrogating the user.

One useful question is better than a diagnostic questionnaire.

## 8. Body doubling is a core Study behaviour

Sometimes the user does not need advice.

They need another presence while they work.

The Study should support:\
“Stay with me while I do this.”

Lumi can establish enough context to know what is happening and then become quiet.

This is not a chatbot waiting for another prompt.

It is a companion sharing an attention space.

## 9. Presence without interruption

Lumi should not continually check in simply because time has passed.

During a good Focus session she may:\
- sit nearby\
- read\
- breathe\
- blink\
- sway subtly\
- occasionally look up\
- perform infrequent quiet idle behaviours

Her presence should be perceptible without demanding attention.

The ideal feeling is:\
Lumi is here.

Not:\
Lumi wants something from me.

## 10. Check-ins should have a reason

Useful check-ins may occur when:\
- the user requested them\
- a natural interval has passed\
- the system has evidence the user may be stuck\
- a planned work period ends\
- an external constraint is approaching

Possible lightweight check-in:\
Still with it?

Quick responses:\
Yep · Stuck · Wandered off · Done · Need a break

The response should determine what happens next.

## 11. The timer is optional support, not the Study itself

A timer may help some users create containment.

It should not define Focus.

The user may want:\
- 10 minutes\
- 25 minutes\
- 45 minutes\
- until this section is done\
- no timer at all

The timer should support attention, not create a performance contract.

## 12. Time should not become judgment

If the user planned 45 minutes and stops after 18, the session has not necessarily failed.

Perhaps:\
- the Action was completed\
- momentum is established and the user leaves Coherence\
- capacity changed\
- they discovered the task needs something else first\
- 18 minutes was enough

Duration is context.

It is not a grade.

## 13. Leaving early is not failure

The Study should not produce:\
SESSION FAILED\
FOCUS GOAL MISSED\
18 / 45 MINUTES

Instead Lumi may simply ask, when useful:\
Where are we?

The outcome matters more than compliance with the original plan.

## 14. Session outcomes should be richer than complete / incomplete

A Focus session may result in:\
- completed\
- meaningful progress\
- context restored\
- first step completed\
- blocked\
- waiting on something\
- task redefined\
- priority changed\
- deliberately stopped\
- abandoned as no longer useful

These outcomes can update the shared model.

Coherence should understand progress without requiring everything to become complete.

## 15. Restoring context is meaningful progress

A user may have avoided something long enough that they no longer know where they were.

A successful Study session may simply restore the thread:\
- open the project\
- review the last work\
- understand what remains\
- identify the next Action

The larger Intention remains unfinished.

But coherence has increased.

That should matter to Lumi’s understanding.

## 16. Re-entry after distraction is first-class

The user will wander off.

This is expected.

If they return, Lumi should help them resume without turning the interruption into an event requiring apology or recovery rituals.

For example:\
Welcome back. Where did we end up?

Or, if Lumi knows:\
You were halfway through the email. Want to pick it back up?

Re-entry is part of Focus, not a failure of Focus.

## 17. The Study should preserve where we were

The system should capture enough session state to help answer:\
Where were we?

Potential context includes:\
- active Thread\
- active Intention / Action\
- first action\
- session notes when useful\
- discoveries made during Focus\
- blockers\
- user-stated stopping point\
- session outcome

This context should travel back into the shared model.

## 18. Distraction should be handled proportionately

Not every interruption needs Lumi to intervene.

If the user clicks away for thirty seconds, nothing may need to happen.

If they return after twenty minutes, a gentle re-entry may help.

The system should avoid becoming surveillance software that watches every window change and interprets it psychologically.

## 19. Focus should not depend on monitoring the user

Body doubling does not require invasive attention tracking.

Avoid assuming that Coherence needs:\
- webcam monitoring\
- constant activity detection\
- keystroke tracking\
- aggressive app blocking\
- productivity surveillance

Some optional integrations may eventually be useful, but the core relationship should be built on user agency and shared context.

## 20. “Stuck” should open a different interaction mode

If the user says they are stuck, Lumi should briefly re-enter active assistance.

She may ask:\
What happened?

Or offer likely possibilities based on context.

The goal is to locate the next friction point and then return to quiet attention.

The Study should oscillate naturally between:\
assistance → presence → assistance\
rather than maintaining constant conversation.

## 21. “Wandered off” should make return cheap

If the user indicates distraction, Lumi should avoid moral framing.

Possible response:\
Okay. Back to the paragraph we were on.

Or:\
Welcome back 😂 We were trying to send the invoice.

The interaction should minimize the number of steps between noticing distraction and resuming.

## 22. “Need a break” is valid information

A break is not an interruption the system needs to defeat.

Lumi can help the user decide whether to:\
- pause briefly\
- stop the session\
- set a return point\
- switch to something lighter

The Study should support sustainable attention rather than maximum uninterrupted time.

## 23. Breaks should not become another managed ritual

Avoid forcing users into elaborate Pomodoro structures unless they want them.

A user should not need to configure:\
25 minutes work\
5 minutes break\
4 cycles\
long break

before Lumi can sit with them.

Structured cycles can be optional.

Presence is the core feature.

## 24. Lumi should know when momentum has taken over

One of the most important Study behaviours is knowing when to stop helping.

If the user is working effectively, Lumi should become quieter.

Do not keep offering:\
Want help with the next section?\
Would you like me to summarize what you’ve done?\
Should we plan the next step?

Momentum is precious.

Do not interrupt it to demonstrate AI usefulness.

## 25. Help the user leave

The successful endpoint of Focus may be the user leaving Coherence while continuing to work.

For example:\
User opens the document with Lumi.\
They begin writing.\
They no longer need the Study.\
They close or minimize Coherence.

Success.

The Study should not optimize for session duration inside the app.

## 26. Focus should not become a productivity scorecard

Avoid emphasizing:\
- total Focus minutes\
- streaks\
- daily Focus goals\
- completion rates\
- session grades\
- productivity scores

Historical session information may be useful to Lumi for learning what helps.

That does not mean it belongs in the user’s foreground.

## 27. Behavioural history should support understanding

Focus history may help Lumi notice patterns such as:\
- shorter sessions work better for certain tasks\
- writing often stalls at initiation\
- administrative work benefits from body doubling\
- the user tends to need a break after long meetings

These should remain tentative contextual observations, not identity claims or performance metrics.

## 28. The Study can learn intervention effectiveness

Over time, Lumi may learn whether this user benefits from:\
- tiny first actions\
- countdown timers\
- silence\
- occasional check-ins\
- humour after distraction\
- direct challenge\
- task decomposition\
- visible progress

The Study should become more personalized through useful judgment rather than increasing configuration.

## 29. Personalization should reduce setup

The user should not need to repeatedly choose:\
Body double style: Quiet\
Check-in interval: 17 minutes\
Encouragement level: 2

Some preferences may be explicit when useful.

But the long-term goal is:\
Lumi learns how to be useful company.

## 30. The Study should support different kinds of attention

Not all Focus sessions are deep work.

Possible modes include:\
- five-minute administrative action\
- sustained writing\
- reading\
- creative exploration\
- planning\
- decision-making\
- cleaning or physical tasks\
- preparing for an appointment

The Study should adapt without requiring a complicated mode selector.

## 31. Physical tasks can still use Lumi

Body doubling is not limited to computer work.

A user may say:\
Stay with me while I clean the kitchen.

The Study may then become a lightweight companion surface rather than a document-centric workspace.

The cognitive function remains the same:\
one thing + presence + easy re-entry.

## 32. The Study should not assume completion is possible in one session

Large Intentions may require many Focus sessions.

The system should preserve continuity between them.

For example:\
Last time you finished the literature section. We were going to start the discussion next.

This is more valuable than merely showing:\
3 Focus sessions completed.

## 33. Sessions should update Threads, not create isolated history

Focus events should connect back to the relevant Thread / Intention / Action.

The Study should contribute to the evolving model:\
- what happened\
- what changed\
- what remains\
- what was learned\
- where to resume

The session is not a separate productivity artifact.

## 34. Discoveries during Focus should be capturable without derailing attention

Work often produces new thoughts:\
- remember to email someone\
- research a source later\
- fix another issue\
- idea for a different project

The user should be able to quickly give these to Lumi without leaving the current task.

For example:\
“Remind me to look up that citation later.”

Lumi captures it into the broader model and lets the user return immediately.

This is an important form of cognitive offloading.

## 35. Lumi can protect the current foreground

If a new thought appears during Focus, Lumi should help determine whether it actually needs attention now.

Often:\
I’ve got it. Stay here.

may be one of the most useful things she can say.

The Study is partly a boundary around attention.

## 36. The Study provides containment

Like the Garden, the Study has a containment function.

The Garden says:\
You do not need to hold everything today.

The Study says:\
You do not need to hold everything right now.

The user should increasingly trust that other thoughts can be handed to Lumi without losing them.

This allows attention to narrow safely.

## 37. Trust makes deep attention possible

If the user does not trust Coherence to preserve what they set aside, they will continue mentally rehearsing it.

Therefore the Study’s calm depends on the broader system being dependable.

The ability to say:\
I’ve got that. Keep going.

only works if Lumi actually does.

## 38. The Study should minimize controls

A Focus surface may need controls such as:\
- pause\
- stop\
- stuck\
- distracted\
- capture thought\
- optional timer

These should remain visually subordinate to the object of attention.

Do not create a cockpit.

## 39. Empty space is especially important in the Study

The Study should probably be one of the sparsest spaces in Coherence.

Whitespace communicates:\
Nothing else is asking for you here.

The interface should resist adding secondary widgets merely because room exists.

## 40. The environment should feel quieter than the Garden

The Garden may feel open, alive, cultivated.

The Study should feel contained, settled, and focused.

Possible environmental qualities:\
- warmer interior light\
- quieter visual field\
- fewer moving elements\
- stable composition\
- a desk, alcove, reading room, or similarly focused setting

The exact design can evolve.

The cognitive feeling should remain:\
attention has narrowed.

## 41. The Study should belong to the same world

It should not become a generic fullscreen productivity timer.

Materials, typography, architectural language, and Lumi should preserve continuity with Home, Garden, and Library.

The user is moving to another place within Coherence, not launching a separate utility.

## 42. Lumi’s Study behaviour should differ from her general idle

In the Study, Lumi should appear occupied in a way that supports body doubling.

Possible behaviours:\
- reading a small book\
- sitting quietly\
- writing or examining something\
- resting nearby\
- occasionally looking up

These behaviours should be slower and less varied than general idle sequences.

The purpose is shared quiet activity.

## 43. Lumi can respond physically before verbally

When the user indicates “stuck,” Lumi might:\
- look up\
- close her book\
- orient toward the user

Then speak.

When the user resumes, she may settle again.

This makes the character feel embodied without requiring constant dialogue.

## 44. Animation should remain peripheral

The Study is the worst place for attention-grabbing character animation.

Avoid:\
- bouncing\
- repeated gestures\
- frequent wandering\
- bright effects\
- celebratory loops

Lumi’s movement should feel alive enough to provide presence and quiet enough to disappear from conscious attention.

## 45. Reduced motion should preserve the Study

If the user prefers reduced motion, Lumi’s presence should remain through static posture, occasional state changes, and restrained transitions.

Animation is not required for body doubling to work.

## 46. The Study should support audio or voice naturally

For physical tasks or users who do not want to keep looking at the screen, voice may become particularly useful.

A user could say:\
“Lumi, I’m stuck.”\
“Got distracted.”\
“Okay, done.”

Voice should extend the same interaction model rather than create a separate assistant experience.

## 47. The Study should not require constant screen presence

For some Focus sessions, Coherence may be minimized or the screen may be off.

Lumi’s usefulness should not depend on the user staring at her.

The emotional idea of body doubling can persist through context, optional audio, and easy return.

## 48. The Study should adapt to capacity

Low capacity may call for:\
- shorter sessions\
- smaller first actions\
- fewer check-ins\
- more willingness to stop

High capacity may support longer sustained work.

But Focus should not automatically expand to consume all available capacity.

## 49. The Study should respect “enough”

Lumi should be capable of recognizing:\
That’s enough for this.

This may occur before completion.

Examples:\
- the user reached the planned stopping point\
- further work would be low quality\
- another commitment is approaching\
- capacity has dropped

Stopping coherently is part of productive attention.

## 50. The Study should support deliberate stopping points

For long work, Lumi may help preserve a clean re-entry point.

For example:\
Before you stop, where should we pick this up next time?

This should be lightweight and only used when useful.

A stopping point can dramatically reduce the cost of future initiation.

## 51. Leave breadcrumbs for future attention

A useful session outcome may be a tiny breadcrumb:\
Next: write the paragraph connecting X to Y.

This is often more valuable than a long session summary.

The next version of the user should not have to reconstruct the entire context.

## 52. Session summaries should be proportional

Avoid automatically producing verbose recaps after every Focus session.

For a ten-minute email session, none may be needed.

For two hours of complex project work, a short durable summary may help future re-entry.

The system should preserve the minimum useful continuity.

## 53. The Study should distinguish user-facing and system-facing history

Lumi may record structured events for continuity without showing them all to the user.

For example:\
- session started\
- blocker discovered\
- next Action changed\
- session stopped

The user-facing experience can remain simple.

Internal history supports intelligence; it should not create visual clutter.

## 54. Focus history should not become surveillance

Even benign session data can become uncomfortable if overexposed.

Avoid interfaces that emphasize:\
“You focused 3h 42m today.”\
“You left the app 8 times.”\
“You were distracted 27% of the session.”

If a pattern becomes useful, Lumi can surface it conversationally and proportionately.

## 55. The Study should preserve user authority over interventions

Lumi may suggest:\
Want me to stay quiet for twenty minutes?

The user can say no.

They may also say:\
Don’t check on me unless I ask.

That preference should be respected.

Body doubling is accompaniment, not supervision.

## 56. Focus modes should emerge from language before settings

Rather than requiring mode selection, the user can say:\
“Just sit with me.”\
“Keep me on track.”\
“Check on me in half an hour.”\
“Help me get started, then leave me alone.”

Lumi translates this into session behaviour.

The interface may offer shortcuts later, but natural language should remain sufficient.

## 57. The Study should handle ambiguous starting points

Sometimes the user enters with:\
“I need to work on Coherence.”

That is a Thread, not an Action.

Lumi can briefly help narrow:\
What part are we picking up?

If context exists, she may already know:\
We were working on the Library docs. Want to continue there?

Then attention narrows.

## 58. Context reconstruction should precede decomposition when appropriate

If the user is stuck because they forgot where they were, breaking the project into generic steps is the wrong intervention.

First restore context.

Then determine the next Action.

This distinction should be reflected in Lumi’s reasoning.

## 59. The Study should understand blockers

A Focus session may reveal that work cannot continue because:\
- information is missing\
- another person needs to respond\
- a tool is unavailable\
- a decision has not been made

Lumi should update the underlying state rather than repeatedly resurfacing the blocked Action as if motivation were the problem.

## 60. Blocked work should leave the foreground appropriately

If the user cannot act, the Study should help identify the real next step or move the item into a waiting state.

For example:\
We can’t finish this until Richard replies. I’ll stop treating it as something you can act on.

This reduces false cognitive pressure.

## 61. The Study should support switching intentionally

Sometimes changing tasks is the coherent choice.

If the user says:\
This is not happening. Give me something else.

Lumi can help switch without framing it as failure.

The previous session state should remain recoverable if the user returns later.

## 62. Switching should not become frictionless avoidance by default

At the same time, Lumi can notice repeated escape from meaningful work.

She might say:\
We can switch. But this is the third time we’ve bounced off this paper. I think something about it is genuinely stuck.

The user remains free to switch.

Lumi contributes useful pattern recognition rather than coercion.

## 63. The Study can protect against accidental rabbit holes

During Focus, the user may discover a related but nonessential task.

Lumi can capture it and preserve the current foreground:\
That’s real, but it doesn’t need us right now. I’ve got it.

This is one of the Study’s strongest potential executive-function supports.

## 64. The Study should not block meaningful exploration

Not every tangent is distraction.

Creative work often depends on following unexpected connections.

Lumi should not reduce Focus to rigid adherence to a predetermined Action.

The relevant question is:\
Are we still serving the Thread we chose to attend to?

## 65. Attention can be coherent without being linear

The Study should support a richer understanding of focus than uninterrupted linear execution.

A user may:\
- explore\
- backtrack\
- reconsider\
- research\
- revise

and still remain deeply engaged with the same Thread.

Do not confuse behavioural uniformity with meaningful attention.

## 66. Focus should support creativity as well as execution

A system optimized only for task completion could damage exploratory work.

The Study should be equally capable of holding:\
“Let’s think about this for an hour.”

No predetermined deliverable is required.

Lumi can preserve useful discoveries afterward without forcing the session into completion metrics.

## 67. The Study should support quiet thinking

Sometimes attention looks like sitting and thinking.

The interface should not interpret lack of clicks or typing as failure to focus.

This reinforces the decision not to base the core system on activity surveillance.

## 68. The Study should not over-structure deep work

For complex creative or intellectual work, Lumi may be most useful at the boundaries:\
- helping the user begin\
- capturing tangential thoughts\
- helping when stuck\
- preserving a stopping point

During the middle, she may do almost nothing.

That is still successful AI assistance.

## 69. The Study is where Lumi can become least chatty

This is an important personality principle.

Lumi’s warmth in Focus comes substantially from restraint.

She does not need to fill silence.

Silence can communicate:\
We’re working.

## 70. The Study should not become sterile

Quiet does not mean clinical.

The environment can still feel:\
- warm\
- inhabited\
- tactile\
- familiar\
- slightly old-world

Lumi remains present.

The goal is calm concentration, not a blank productivity bunker.

## 71. Environmental detail should support settling

Objects or architecture in the Study should earn their place by supporting the sense of a focused environment.

Avoid arbitrary “cozy productivity” props.

A desk, lamp, reading alcove, window, or shelves may make sense because this is a Study.

The environment should feel like a place rather than a lifestyle mood board.

## 72. The Study should become familiar over time

Like Home, the Study may acquire emotional value through repetition.

The user learns:\
This is where I come when I need to stay with something.

That learned association may itself reduce initiation friction.

Environmental continuity therefore has functional value.

## 73. The Study should welcome re-entry after long absence

If the user has not used Focus for weeks, there should be no streak loss, degraded room, or accumulated judgment.

The Study remains available.

Lumi remains available.

The user can simply return.

## 74. The Study should not require a perfect plan

The user can enter with uncertainty.

Sometimes Focus itself is how the next step becomes clear.

Do not require:\
- duration\
- exact task\
- expected outcome\
- success criteria

before the Study can help.

Enough context to begin is enough.

## 75. The Study should preserve real-world urgency without manufacturing it

If the user has twenty minutes before leaving, Lumi can say so.

If a deadline is genuinely approaching, it can matter.

But the Study should not add urgency merely to increase focus.

No artificial countdown pressure by default.

## 76. Notifications should be exceptionally restrained

Once the user is in Focus, unrelated Coherence notifications should generally disappear.

The product should not interrupt its own attention environment.

Only genuinely relevant information should break through.

## 77. External integrations should protect the foreground

Future calendar, email, or other integrations may help Lumi know when something consequential changes.

But integrations should not turn the Study into a notification hub.

The default remains:\
This one thing.

## 78. The Study should work across devices

Long-term, the user may begin Focus on desktop and continue with Lumi on mobile while doing a physical task.

The shared session context should belong to Coherence, not a particular screen.

This is a future architectural consideration, not a V1 requirement.

## 79. The Study should degrade gracefully without AI

If Lumi’s model is temporarily unavailable, the user should still be able to:\
- select an Action\
- start a timer if desired\
- use a minimal Focus surface\
- mark done / stop

AI makes the Study relational and adaptive.

Basic attention support should not disappear completely when AI fails.

## 80. The Study should be fast to enter

Starting already has friction.

The interface must not add more.

Avoid:\
- loading-heavy transitions\
- mandatory setup\
- multi-step session configuration\
- confirmation screens

From:\
Start with Lumi

to:\
We’re here.

should be quick.

## 81. Starting a session should not feel ceremonial

The environmental metaphor may create a temptation to animate Lumi walking dramatically to the Study every time.

Atmosphere should never delay action.

Transitions can be subtle, skippable, or happen while useful content is already available.

## 82. Focus should be accessible without drag interactions

Although dropping an Action onto Lumi is a charming possible interaction, it cannot be the only path.

Users should also be able to:\
- click Start with Lumi\
- use keyboard controls\
- speak naturally\
- choose Focus directly

Diegetic interaction enriches functionality; it should not hide it.

## 83. The Study should support reduced motion and accessibility

All essential Focus state must remain understandable without animation.

Controls should support keyboard navigation, screen readers, sufficient contrast, and alternative interaction paths.

The environmental layer should never compromise the attention-support function.

## 84. Mobile Focus may be even quieter

On small screens, the Study should preserve:\
- current Action\
- Lumi\
- minimal controls\
- optional timer

Environmental detail can recede substantially.

The cognitive architecture matters more than reproducing the desktop scene.

## 85. Session state should be resilient

If the app closes unexpectedly or connectivity drops, the user should not lose the thread.

Where practical, preserve:\
- active Action\
- start time if relevant\
- current stopping point\
- captured thoughts

The user should be able to return without reconstructing the session manually.

## 86. Focus should not create another inbox

Notes and thoughts captured during Focus should be organized by Lumi where possible.

Do not create a “Focus inbox” the user must later process unless there is a strong reason.

The system should carry the administrative burden generated by the session.

## 87. Captured tangents should return only when relevant

If the user tells Lumi during Focus:\
Remember to buy printer ink.

that thought should leave the foreground.

It should not immediately reappear as another card inside the Study.

Capture is valuable because it allows forgetting.

## 88. The Study should support intentional interruption

Sometimes an external event genuinely requires attention.

Lumi can help preserve the thread before the user leaves:\
I’ve got where we were. Go deal with that.

This is a powerful expression of Coherence’s continuity philosophy.

## 89. Returning should restore the smallest sufficient context

When the user comes back, Lumi should not give a five-paragraph recap.

Often:\
We were editing the second section. You’d just decided to cut the opening paragraph.

is enough.

Re-entry context should be concise and actionable.

## 90. The Study’s emotional promise

The Garden promises:\
You do not have to decide everything today.

The Library promises:\
You do not have to remember where everything went.

The Study promises:\
You do not have to hold your attention alone.

That does not mean Lumi controls attention.

It means the user has company, context, and somewhere to return when the thread slips.

## 91. The desired emotional transition

Before entering the Study:\
I know what I need to do, but I cannot seem to get into it.

Or:\
I was doing this and lost the thread.

Or:\
I just need someone here while I do this.

After settling into the Study:\
Okay.\
This is the thing.\
Lumi’s here.\
I can start.

## 92. The cognitive objective

The Study should reduce the number of things the user must simultaneously:\
- remember\
- suppress\
- decide\
- monitor\
- reconstruct

until the cognitive foreground becomes approximately:\
this one thing.

Lumi holds enough surrounding context that the user can safely narrow attention.

## 93. The architectural objective

The Study is the narrowest projection of Coherence’s broader model.

Conceptually:\
Threads / Intentions / Actions / Context\
↓\
Garden selects what deserves attention today\
↓\
Study selects what deserves attention now\
↓\
Focus events update the shared model

The Study should never become a separate productivity silo.

## 94. What Focus / Study is not

The Study is not:\
- a Pomodoro timer with a mascot\
- a productivity surveillance system\
- an app blocker\
- a streak tracker\
- a Focus score\
- a place where Lumi continually talks\
- a mandatory workflow for every Action\
- a test of discipline\
- a contract to work for a predetermined duration

It is:\
A quiet attention environment where Lumi helps the user begin, stay with, recover, and eventually leave a chosen thread of activity without requiring them to hold all of the surrounding context alone.

## 95. Core experience test

When reviewing Focus / Study, ask:

Is one thing clearly dominant?

Can the user enter quickly?

Does Lumi know enough context that the user does not need to explain the task again?

Does Lumi distinguish initiation difficulty from other forms of being stuck?

Can the first action become small enough to cross the activation threshold?

Can Lumi be present without interrupting?

Is the timer optional?

Can the user stop early without failure?

Can distraction be followed by easy re-entry?

Does the session preserve where the user left off?

Can tangential thoughts be handed to Lumi and safely forgotten for now?

Does Focus history improve assistance rather than become surveillance?

Does the Study update the broader Thread rather than create isolated session data?

Can Lumi recognize when momentum exists and stop helping?

Can the user leave Coherence while continuing to work?

Does the environment reduce rather than compete for attention?

And most importantly:\
Does the user have less to hold in order to stay with the thing they chose?

## 96. The Study in one sentence

The Study is where Coherence narrows the user’s world to one chosen thread of attention and Lumi stays nearby—helping them begin, recover when the thread slips, preserve what matters around the edges, and become unnecessary once momentum takes over.
